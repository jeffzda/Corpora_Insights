What is a Knowledge Sharing Plan? 
As part of your ARENA application, you are required to submit a proposed Knowledge Sharing Plan 
(KSP), which will form the basis of the knowledge sharing deliverables in the funding agreement. This 
document outlines how your project will share insights, lessons learned, and outcomes with key 
stakeholders.  
Your KSP must be:  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
A well-structured KSP helps maximise the impact of ARENA’s funding by ensuring knowledge is 
accessible and useful to others working in the sector. 
 
Your commitment 
Knowledge is ARENA’s return on investment. To receive ARENA funding, Recipients must fulfill 
knowledge sharing deliverables at various stages of the project. These deliverables vary based 
on project type and may be one-off or recurring, tied to specific milestones or dates.  
We remain aware of the rapidly evolving nature of the industry, and work with you to ensure that the 
content is both relevant and timely. 
 
Frequently Asked Questions 
What is ARENA doing with commercially sensitive data? 
Sensitive data is collected and processed in an aggregated 
format to support portfolio analysis. All data by ARENA is 
stored in secure Australian data centers. 
How detailed should my KSP be? 
It should be clear, practical, and outline key lessons, target 
audiences, and estimated timeframes for delivery.  
When should knowledge be shared? 
At critical points - such as project setup, mid-project 
updates, and completion - when it’s most relevant and 
impactful.

Knowledge sharing does not require 
you to compromise commercially

sharing industry-relevant insights and

business’s proprietary interests.

Final Knowledge Sharing Report  
A public Final Knowledge Sharing Report is 
required at project completion, summarising key 
findings, lessons learnt, future applications, and 
other agreed topics. 
Report structure: 
> 
Cover page 
> 
Index of tables/figures (if applicable) 
> 
Executive summary (key points, 
conclusions, recommendations) 
> 
Introduction/background 
> 
Project insights/outcomes (with images and 
lists where appropriate) 
> 
Conclusion (key findings, industry 
implications, recommendations, next steps) 
> 
References/appendices (if needed)

performance metrics. A consistent dataset

wide analysis. Most required data is already 
collected by projects or publicly available with 
best-practice monitoring.

timely insights. These reports should include 
achievements, challenges, and solutions on

social aspects of the project.
