# Solar thermal — Delivery Risk Profile

## Executive Summary

Solar thermal projects face fundamentally different risk patterns than other renewable technologies. Where PV and wind compete primarily on LCOE, solar thermal's value proposition rests on dispatchability and thermal storage — capabilities that current market structures struggle to reward. This creates a commercial paradox: the technology works technically but lacks sufficient economic signals for deployment.

The evidence shows three dominant failure modes. **Technical underperformance** affects 30% of adverse outcomes, driven primarily by first-of-kind receiver designs, materials compatibility issues with high-temperature fluids like sodium, and integration challenges between thermal storage and power cycles. **Commercial & market failures** hit 22% of projects with disproportionate severity (60% escalate to major/critical) — policy uncertainty, financing gaps for FOAK technology, and competition from rapidly declining PV costs systematically undermine project economics. **Execution & logistics problems** plague 16% of records, with regional Australian deployment particularly affected by skills shortages, supply chain immaturity, and extended construction timelines.

Most critically, solar thermal technology development sits in limbo between demonstration and commercial deployment. The Jemalong CSP pilot demonstrated technical feasibility but required extensive receiver redesign. The Kogan Creek Solar Boost project collapsed due to vendor exit and supply chain failures. Even successful projects like RayGen's SPP1 achieve technical milestones but warn that commercial deployment requires multiple projects to drive learning curves and supply chain development.

Project managers should expect 30-month construction timelines, significant regional cost premiums, and financing structures requiring anchor offtakers and government co-funding. The technology is not inherently flawed but requires sustained policy commitment and pipeline development that Australia has not yet provided.

## The Evidence Base

This profile draws from 587 delivery records spanning 31 projects from 2012-2024, with strong temporal coverage across demonstration, pilot, and early commercial phases. The dataset includes major Australian initiatives: ASTRI's comprehensive R&D program (2012-2023), the failed Kogan Creek Solar Boost project (discontinued 2016), RayGen's successful technology progression from R&D through SPP1 commissioning (2024), and Vast Solar's Jemalong pilot plant development (2013-2019).

Coverage is strongest in design and integration challenges (258 design records, 51 integration/commissioning records), reflecting the technical complexity of thermal systems. Construction records are fewer but severe (57 records with 63% execution failures), indicating that reaching construction represents a significant filter but does not guarantee completion.

41 records (7%) carry temporal warnings indicating pre-2021 insights about fast-moving technology or market conditions. These primarily relate to cost comparisons with PV (since overtaken) and early policy frameworks. However, governance, coordination, and materials challenges show strong time-stability across the dataset.

Key gaps include limited operational data from commercial-scale plants (reflecting deployment delays) and sparse representation of international supply chains outside of specific vendor relationships (AREVA, RayGen partners).

## Where Things Go Wrong

### Technical Underperformance: The Receiver Problem

Technical underperformance dominates the failure spectrum, affecting 30% of adverse outcomes and spanning the full technology lifecycle from design through operations. The evidence reveals a consistent pattern: **receiver technology is the critical failure point**.

The Jemalong pilot plant's receiver "went through three distinct design iterations that were each fabricated, installed and tested at the plant over the course of construction and commissioning." Following grid connection in January 2017, "receiver re-design and recommissioning work took place throughout 2017, meaning that electricity was not sent to the grid again until early 2018" — a 12-month gap between first power and reliable generation.

Similarly, the Kogan Creek project suffered catastrophic receiver material failures: "The majority of economiser boiler tubes rapidly corroded both before and after installation. These economiser boiler tubes were 'Solonyx' coated tubes, which was a coating developed by AREVA Solar. AREVA Solar discontinued production of this coating shortly after completion of the tube production run."

The pattern extends beyond pilot projects. ASTRI's research on high-temperature receivers found that "current state-of-the-art surround heliostat fields with CPC secondary concentrators limit receiver operating temperature to approximately 1,300°C, constraining storage temperature to ~1,200°C," preventing next-generation CSP systems from reaching their efficiency targets.

Materials compatibility emerges as the underlying technical driver. Testing of supercritical CO₂ systems revealed that "conditions under which Supercritical CO₂ is required to operate result in short life spans for traditional seal materials," with "PTFE-based compression packings and polymer anti-extrusion rings" deteriorating rapidly. For sodium-based systems, "data regarding the compatibility of sodium with containment materials, particularly high-nickel alloys, was scarce at the high temperatures targeted."

### Commercial & Market: The Policy-Technology Mismatch

Commercial failures affect 22% of adverse records but escalate to major/critical severity in 60% of cases — the highest escalation rate across all failure modes. The core issue is structural: solar thermal's primary value (dispatchability through thermal storage) is not rewarded by existing market mechanisms.

"CST LCOE is universally acknowledged to be higher than PV or wind without storage, and current renewable energy support mechanisms such as Australia's RET reward all renewable MWhs equally, providing no additional value for the dispatchability, firm capacity, or system stability benefits that CST with thermal storage provides."

This creates a systematic financing barrier. In the 2017 RFI process, "none of the 31 RFI respondents advocated that CST project developers build plants to operate on a merchant basis; all assumed that long-term income certainty via some offtake mechanism is required before a commercial-scale CST plant can be financed."

The policy uncertainty compounds technology risk. Vast Solar found that "Australian financial and utility markets are highly conservative and risk averse toward unproven CSP technology," with "discussions with Australian merchant and investment banks" indicating "that Vast Solar's capacity to raise debt is negligible" because "the financial sector will only consider technology investments when the technology is demonstrably proven in several plants."

The competitive environment deteriorated during long development cycles. Kogan Creek was "conceived in 2009, when the levelised cost of electricity from solar thermal generation was comparable to that of solar photovoltaic generation. However, over the intervening period, solar photovoltaic plants have significantly reduced their costs compared to solar thermal plants," eliminating the commercial case for the technology.

### Execution & Logistics: Regional Australia Reality

Execution challenges affect 16% of records but reveal systematic barriers to deployment in Australia's high-solar regions. The Jemalong pilot provides a comprehensive case study of regional deployment challenges:

**Skills and Labour**: "Recruiting and retaining qualified project engineers during construction was a challenge due in part to the competitive marketplace for engineering talent in regional Australia." At construction peak, "qualified high-pressure welders were scarce in the Forbes region and extremely expensive due to competition from concurrent mining and Queensland LNG gas booms."

**Supply Chain Dependencies**: "The lack of industrial supply chain partners — regionally and nationally — forced Vast Solar to source most specialist materials and knowledge from overseas (primarily Europe and Asia), adding timezone delays, extended shipping lead times, prototype fabrication waits, and higher freight costs." Even standard materials like "Schedule 80 High Pressure Pipes — could be difficult and expensive to procure" with "surprised by stock lead times and availability from within Australia."

**Infrastructure Deficits**: The region experienced "unreliable and very slow internet connectivity throughout construction and commissioning," requiring "multiple interim investments in repeaters, antenna extensions and alternative service providers" before "ultimately, the issue was solved by line-of-sight NBN from Forbes and Parkes via a costly and custom installation."

**Natural Hazards**: "In September 2016 the Forbes Greater Region experienced what is now generally considered to have been a very significant, 1 in 100 year flood. All major regional roads were closed for a period of 2-4 weeks" during "the final stages of construction," forcing project shutdown.

The cumulative effect is substantial cost premiums. SPP2 cost estimates of A$749–826 million "reflect a significant remote-location cost premium due to high labour rates and logistics challenges, with RayGen noting these costs are above those that would be encountered at most other project sites in Australia or globally."

## Failure Mode Deep-Dives

### Technical Underperformance: Beyond the Lab

Solar thermal technical challenges differ qualitatively from other renewable technologies because the systems operate at extreme temperatures with novel heat transfer fluids and require integration across multiple subsystems (solar field, thermal storage, power block) that must function as a coordinated whole.

**Receiver Design Iteration Cycles**: The Jemalong experience demonstrates that receiver design cannot be frozen early. Even after "extensive receiver and heliostat field testing at various scales and configurations" during development, "initial receiver design was found to be sub-optimal following commissioning in early 2017, requiring re-engineering and re-design of the receivers and associated protection systems."

The BRAAC project reached similar conclusions: "The receiver module built and tested in this project was extremely complex, resulting in a long design period, and consequent time pressure during the testing phase." The finding was that "simplicity should be a priority for future designs, since this also implies savings in the final product."

**Materials Compatibility at Scale**: Laboratory materials testing fails to capture the full operating envelope of solar thermal systems. The sCO₂ testing revealed that "while significant information exists for carbon dioxide compatibility on most materials at normal pressures and temperatures, under supercritical conditions, CO₂ becomes a solvent that can attack most seal materials."

Similarly, high-temperature chloride salt storage "required an inert gas environment maintained in a closed system to achieve good thermal stability above 700°C. This represents a significant operational constraint not present in conventional nitrate salt systems."

**Integration Complexity**: System-level integration represents a distinct failure mode beyond component performance. RayGen's SPP1 commissioning took 11 months after construction completion specifically for "integration of control systems, dispatch logic, and grid interaction" — a distinct phase requiring dedicated resources.

The pattern is that "components sourced from international suppliers deteriorated faster than expected under operating conditions of 650°C and 20 MPa," meaning that "industry is currently underprepared to supply components for the sCO₂ power cycle that will meet the longevity and performance requirements a commercial plant."

### Commercial & Market: The Valley of Death at Scale

Solar thermal's commercial challenges operate across multiple dimensions simultaneously, creating compound failure modes that individual risk mitigation strategies cannot address.

**Financing Structure Misalignment**: The technology requires capital-intensive upfront investment with revenue streams that current market structures do not adequately reward. "For FOAK technology with unproven core components (such as sodium receivers), plan for multiple design-build-test iterations during construction and commissioning — schedule and cost contingency must accommodate at least two full rework cycles for the highest-risk novel elements."

This is compounded by scale requirements. "CST projects require larger minimum scale than wind or PV (broadly >50 MWe) due to steam turbine efficiency scaling," meaning projects cannot start small and scale up incrementally.

**Technology Provider Concentration Risk**: The Kogan Creek failure illustrates a systematic risk in the sector: "When the EPC contractor is the sole technology provider for a first-of-a-kind commercial solar thermal project, the proponent must explicitly assess and monitor the contractor's financial and strategic commitment to the technology — AREVA's corporate decision to exit the CSP market rendered completion impossible."

This pattern extends beyond individual projects. "Experienced CST respondents identified that regulatory and policy uncertainty in Australia — including uncertainty around the RET and broader energy and climate policy — represents a significant risk premium applied to CST project costs and financing."

**Competitive Technology Evolution**: Solar thermal faces a unique competitive dynamic where its primary rival (PV) continues rapid cost reduction during the multi-year development cycles required for thermal plants. "The project's commercial viability was eroded by the rapid and unanticipated decline in solar PV costs between project conception in 2009 and project execution."

### Execution & Logistics: The Regional Penalty

Regional deployment in Australia's high-solar areas creates compounding logistical challenges that urban-focused engineering approaches systematically underestimate.

**Skills Ecosystem Breakdown**: Regional areas lack the technical skills ecosystem required for complex industrial construction. The Jemalong project found that "general construction labour sourced through local labour hire companies was of variable quality and could not reliably commit to multi-month engagements. This forced Vast Solar's already stretched technical supervisory staff to invest repetitive time in training and overseeing incoming labourers."

The problem extends to specialist trades: "Vast Solar was unable to source this critical skill domestically at acceptable cost" for high-pressure welders and "addressed this challenge by utilising skilled foreign workers" through 457 Visa processes.

**Supply Chain Fragmentation**: "Even relatively standard construction materials — specifically Schedule 80 high-pressure pipes — were difficult and expensive to procure within Australia, with unexpectedly long stock lead times." For specialist equipment, "unique sensors from Germany or Holland can be sourced from Australian retailers — but with 4–6 weeks lead time" and "some materials (e.g. high-temperature thermal paste) had no Australian manufacturer, importer or retailer at all."

**Infrastructure Dependencies**: Telecommunications emerged as a systematic constraint: "The Jemalong region is one of New South Wales's many blackspots resulting in unreliable coverage. Various interim solutions were explored and following numerous investments throughout 2016 and 2017… Ultimately, the issue was solved by line-of-sight NBN from Forbes and Parkes via a costly and custom installation."

Emergency services capabilities also lag technology requirements: "Sodium as a heat transfer fluid was relatively unknown to Australian fire and emergency services personnel, creating a safety knowledge gap that needed to be actively managed during pilot plant development and operation."

## What Has Changed Over Time

The solar thermal risk profile has evolved significantly across the 2012-2024 timeframe, with distinct patterns of improvement and deterioration.

**Technical Risks: Improvement Through R&D Investment**

ASTRI's systematic R&D program (2012-2023) generated substantial learning on materials compatibility and system integration. Early projects struggled with basic materials issues: "The majority of economiser boiler tubes rapidly corroded both before and after installation" at Kogan Creek (2016). By contrast, recent projects show more sophisticated materials approaches: "ASTRI's materials program identified that the cost impact of using superalloys in high-temperature CSP systems could be substantially mitigated by using less material to achieve the same strength outcome."

Receiver technology has also matured. While the Jemalong receiver "went through three distinct design iterations" during commissioning (2017-2018), RayGen's SPP1 achieved "consistent and reliable operation" by 2024, though still required an 11-month integrated commissioning period.

**Commercial Risks: Systematic Deterioration**

The competitive position of solar thermal has deteriorated significantly as PV costs collapsed faster than CSP technology could improve. The 2017 RFI noted that "CST LCOE is universally acknowledged to be higher than PV or wind without storage." By 2024, RayGen's SPP1 analysis found that even with demonstrated technical success, "FOAK project cost benchmarks established during major global disruptions (e.g., COVID-19) should be disaggregated to isolate pandemic-related cost premiums before using them as reference-class estimates for subsequent projects."

Policy support has remained fragmented. Early projects operated under frameworks where "current renewable energy support mechanisms such as Australia's RET reward all renewable MWhs equally, providing no additional value for the dispatchability." Recent policy developments have not fundamentally addressed this gap, though there is recognition that "ensuring policy and market settings enable long duration hybrid plants to capture their true value to the system is critical."

**Supply Chain and Skills: Mixed Progress**

Regional deployment capabilities show mixed evolution. The Jemalong project (2013-2019) faced severe skills shortages: "recruiting and retaining qualified project engineers during construction was a challenge." Recent projects continue to identify skills as a constraint, but with more sophisticated solutions: RayGen's approach includes "engaging an experienced industrial partner before designing a scaled-up production line" to compress learning curves.

International supply chain relationships have become more structured. Where early projects suffered from "immaturity of AREVA Solar's global 'least cost' supply chain," recent projects like RayGen have established "strategic deployment agreement with SLB (formerly Schlumberger) to provide technology development, scale-up, manufacturing, strategic sourcing, and global market access capabilities."

**Emerging Risk Patterns**

Grid connection complexity has emerged as a new risk category. Early projects faced standard utility connection processes, but hybrid solar-plus-storage systems now encounter systematic barriers: "Hybrid solar-plus-storage plants should anticipate that standard network connection processes are not designed for them — budget for additional modelling iterations and engage the NSP early."

Integration complexity has also escalated. While early single-technology demonstrations could validate component performance, recent projects reveal that "for novel multi-subsystem plants, budget significant additional time (potentially 6–12 months) between individual subsystem commissioning and full integrated commissioning."

## Due Diligence Checklist

### Commercial and Financing Viability

**Question**: Does the project have a confirmed anchor offtaker with a creditworthy counterparty for at least 10 years?
**Red flag**: Reliance on merchant revenue or PPA processes "under development." All bankable CST projects in the dataset required anchor offtakers before financial close.
**Why it matters**: "None of the 31 RFI respondents advocated that CST project developers build plants to operate on a merchant basis" — CST cannot be financed on merchant terms.

**Question**: What is the LCOE comparison with PV-plus-battery at the same site for equivalent dispatch capability?
**Red flag**: LCOE comparisons that don't include storage to match CST's dispatchability.
**Why it matters**: CST's value is dispatchability, not raw energy cost. If PV-plus-battery delivers equivalent services at lower cost, the CST business case collapses.

**Question**: How much government co-funding is assumed, and is it contractually committed?
**Red flag**: Relying on "policy support under development" or assuming grant funding without formal commitments.
**Why it matters**: "First-commercial CSF plants are unlikely to attract purely private investment at standard equity return thresholds without subsidy" — this applies equally to CST.

### Technology and Execution Readiness

**Question**: What is the technology provider's track record of multi-year commercial operation (not just commissioning) at comparable scale?
**Red flag**: Technology providers with pilot-scale experience only, or whose most recent project required extended commissioning or redesign.
**Why it matters**: "The financial sector will only consider technology investments when the technology is demonstrably proven in several plants" — single demonstration projects are insufficient for bankability.

**Question**: Has the receiver design been validated under the specific operating conditions (temperature, thermal cycling, HTF chemistry) planned for this project?
**Red flag**: Receivers designed for different operating conditions, or materials selection based on laboratory rather than field data.
**Why it matters**: Receiver failures are the dominant technical risk, typically requiring 6-18 months of redesign and recommissioning.

**Question**: What is the contingency allowance for integrated commissioning after mechanical completion?
**Red flag**: Commissioning schedules that don't distinguish mechanical completion from integrated operational readiness.
**Why it matters**: "For novel multi-subsystem plants, budget significant additional time (potentially 6–12 months) between individual subsystem commissioning and full integrated commissioning."

### Site and Supply Chain Risk

**Question**: Has the site-specific wind data been collected for at least 24 months, and what are the seasonal extremes?
**Red flag**: Reliance on regional weather data or wind estimates from other sites.
**Why it matters**: "There is no single optimum heliostat design for all sites, as each site has unique wind and soil conditions" — conservative safety factors inflate costs until site-specific data is available.

**Question**: What is the distance to the nearest supplier of specialist equipment hire (cranes, high-voltage electrical specialists)?
**Red flag**: Remote sites with no local access to specialist construction equipment.
**Why it matters**: "Specialist construction equipment was not available from hire companies in Forbes itself; the nearest suppliers were in Orange and Parkes" — transport and coordination costs compound.

**Question**: Are critical suppliers committed to long-term support, or are any using proprietary components that could be discontinued?
**Red flag**: Reliance on single-supplier proprietary components, especially from companies with limited CST portfolios.
**Why it matters**: "AREVA Solar discontinued production of this coating shortly after completion of the tube production run" — proprietary component discontinuation can strand projects.

### Grid and Regulatory Pathway

**Question**: Has the network operator confirmed that their connection process can accommodate hybrid storage-generation systems?
**Red flag**: Standard generation connection processes that haven't been updated for hybrid systems.
**Why it matters**: "Hybrid solar-plus-storage plants should anticipate that standard network connection processes are not designed for them" — budget additional modelling iterations.

**Question**: How many years of local DNI measurement data are available from the specific site?
**Red flag**: Reliance on satellite data or modelled estimates without ground-based validation.
**Why it matters**: "Insufficiently accurate DNI data" is identified internationally as "a material barrier to financing" — investment-grade data requires multi-year ground-based measurement.

**Question**: Are emergency services trained in the specific HTF hazards, and are appropriate response procedures established?
**Red flag**: Novel HTFs (sodium, high-temperature oils) without established local emergency response protocols.
**Why it matters**: "Sodium as a heat transfer fluid was relatively unknown to Australian fire and emergency services personnel, creating a safety knowledge gap" — requires proactive training and protocol development.
