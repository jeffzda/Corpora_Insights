# Solar thermal — Delivery Risk Profile

## Executive summary

Solar thermal projects face uniquely severe commercialisation challenges in Australia, with technical underperformance and commercial/market failures dominating the risk landscape. Early ARENA projects experienced fundamental technical barriers (receivers requiring multiple redesigns, sodium fires, complex software failures), while newer projects reveal that even technically successful demonstrations struggle with uneconomic LCOH, limited offtake markets, and conservative financing conditions. Portfolio managers should expect extended development timelines, multiple technical iterations, and challenging commercial negotiations as standard for this technology category.

## Coverage and data quality

This profile draws on 587 records from 31 projects spanning 2012–2025+. Temporal flags apply to 41 records (7%), primarily from 2015–2018 when several high-profile CSP projects (Aurora, SolarFlagship) were abandoned. The profile balances historical context from early demonstration projects with recent findings from ongoing R&D programs. Data quality is strong for technical failure modes but weaker for contemporary commercial conditions, as most recent projects remain in development rather than operation.

## Risk landscape by delivery dimension

**Design** emerges as the highest-risk dimension (258 adverse records), driven primarily by technical underperformance (42% of design issues). Solar thermal projects consistently encounter fundamental design challenges: receivers require multiple iterations during commissioning, novel heat transfer fluids (sodium, sCO2) create materials compatibility issues, and integration between concentrator and receiver systems proves more complex than anticipated. The Jemalong pilot plant exemplifies this pattern—receiver designs went through three complete fabrication cycles during construction, each requiring installation and testing.

**Financing** follows closely (81 records), with commercial/market failures accounting for 69% of financing issues. Even technically successful projects struggle with LCOH significantly above fossil fuel alternatives—early CST air heating systems showed LCOH of AUD59.5/GJ versus AUD10/GJ for natural gas. The Aurora CSP project's abandonment due to "financial hurdles" and Vast Solar's finding that Australian banks consider their debt capacity "negligible" illustrate systematic financing challenges.

**Procurement** (134 records) faces dual challenges: technical underperformance in equipment selection (28%) and execution/logistics problems (31%). Australia lacks domestic CSP supply chains, forcing reliance on international suppliers with extended lead times and coordination difficulties. The Chromasun field failure—where vendor cessation left operators without support for complex tracking systems—demonstrates procurement risks when selecting commercially marginal technologies.

**Operations** reflects the compound effect of design and procurement problems, with technical underperformance (36%) and data/measurement issues (31%) dominating. The Chromasun field operated on only 30% of available days over two years, achieving 115°C outlet temperatures against a 170°C design. Manual operation systems reduced solar utilization to 40-50% of available hours at Echuca hospital, while complex software caused cascading failures across multiple collector strings.

## Failure mode deep-dive

**Technical underperformance** (30% of all adverse records) manifests as fundamental design challenges rather than minor performance shortfalls. Novel components consistently require multiple design iterations: particle receivers experience clogging, leaks, and thermal expansion issues; sodium receivers undergo three complete redesigns during commissioning; complex tracking systems fail to achieve design temperatures. The pattern suggests systematic underestimation of engineering complexity for high-temperature, multi-component systems. Recent projects show this continues—even successful demonstrations like RayGen's SPP1 required 11 months of integrated commissioning after individual subsystems were complete.

**Commercial & market failures** (22% of records, 60% severity escalation) reflect structural challenges beyond technical readiness. The Australian market demonstrates profound conservatism: banks require technology proven "across several operating plants" before considering investment, utilities are "unwilling to engage with prospective new technologies," and PPAs remain inaccessible for FOAK deployments. High capital costs create uneconomic LCOH—windowed CST for industrial heat achieved AUD27.1/GJ compared to AUD10/GJ for natural gas, requiring 40% capital cost reductions to reach viability.

**Execution & logistics** (16% of records) problems cascade from supply chain and coordination challenges. International component sourcing adds "timezone delays, extended shipping lead times, prototype fabrication waits" throughout projects. COVID-19 impacts were particularly severe for construction-intensive solar thermal, with "market conditions driving up materials and labour costs." The absence of domestic expertise requires overseas specialist support, complicating troubleshooting and maintenance.

**Coordination & stakeholders** (9% of records) issues often involve safety and regulatory complexities. Sodium fires require specialist emergency response from Sydney to rural sites; grid connection processes aren't designed for novel hybrid technologies; local fire services lack capability to manage reactive materials. These coordination challenges extend timelines and increase costs beyond technical development needs.

## Temporal trends

Early projects (2012–2018) suffered acute technical failures and commercial abandonments—multiple high-profile CSP projects were cancelled, pilot plants required extensive receiver redesigns, and complex collector systems proved unreliable. The sector appeared to stabilize around 2019 as projects shifted toward lower-risk approaches: established molten salt systems, off-sun reactor operation, and hybrid configurations reducing solar dependence.

Recent projects (2022+) show more sophisticated technical approaches but persistent commercial challenges. RayGen successfully integrated PV and thermal storage, achieving technical milestones and securing commercial offtake with AGL. However, even this success required pivoting from solar-only to hybrid storage configuration in response to market feedback. Current CST industrial heat projects identify paths to technical viability but remain uneconomic without substantial capital cost reductions or higher gas prices.

The pattern suggests technical risk has become more manageable through conservative design choices and extensive R&D, but commercial viability remains elusive at current costs and market conditions.

## Key watchpoints for due diligence

1. **Receiver complexity and iteration risk**: For any novel receiver design, require evidence of full-scale operational testing under design conditions. Budget 2-3 complete design-build-test cycles for FOAK receivers, and verify that project schedules accommodate potential re-engineering during commissioning.

2. **Materials compatibility validation**: High-temperature systems using novel heat transfer fluids (sodium, sCO2, thermal storage media) must demonstrate materials compatibility through dedicated testing. Generic engineering assumptions about alloy performance have historically proven inadequate and created downstream failures.

3. **Supply chain dependency assessment**: Map critical component sourcing and vendor support continuity. Australian projects dependent on single international suppliers face material execution risk if vendors withdraw support or cease trading. Verify backup suppliers or in-house capability for complex subsystems.

4. **Integration systems engineering**: Evaluate integration complexity between concentrators, receivers, storage, and power conversion subsystems. Projects scoping only individual components without system-level optimization have consistently underestimated integration costs and performance penalties.

5. **Commercial pathway validation**: Require explicit demonstration of offtake market engagement and financing term sheets. Australian utilities and banks have demonstrated systematic reluctance to engage with FOAK CSP, making revenue assumptions based on general market projections unreliable.

6. **Safety and emergency response planning**: For technologies using reactive materials, verify that local emergency services have appropriate training and equipment. Sodium fires and similar incidents require specialist response capabilities that may not exist in rural project locations.

7. **LCOH benchmarking against alternatives**: Validate that projected LCOH accounts for all integration costs and compares realistically against incumbent energy sources. Recent industrial heat projects show LCOH 2-6x higher than natural gas, requiring explicit cost reduction pathways or policy support to achieve viability.
