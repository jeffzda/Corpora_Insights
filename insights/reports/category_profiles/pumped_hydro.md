# Pumped hydro — Delivery Risk Profile

## Executive Summary

Pumped hydro energy storage (PHES) projects in Australia face a perfect storm of delivery challenges: complex multi-year approvals, extreme capital cost escalation, limited contractor capability, and structural market barriers that make traditional project finance nearly impossible. **The evidence is stark: no private PHES project except Snowy 2.0 has reached FID in Australia in recent years, despite multiple serious attempts backed by major utilities.**

Three critical patterns dominate the failure modes. First, **capital costs systematically exceed feasibility estimates by 50%+ once EPC tenders are received** — Origin's Shoalhaven expansion escalated from feasibility estimates to $570-630M ($2.5M/MW), while EnergyAustralia's Cultana project hit $790.5M ($3.4M/MW), both far above international benchmarks. Second, **geological uncertainty drives the highest contingency requirements** of any technology in ARENA's portfolio — underground works encounter unexpected water ingress, high horizontal stresses, and fractured rock that force major design changes mid-construction. Third, **revenue uncertainty interacts toxically with 5+ year development timelines** — by the time projects reach FID, competing storage (Snowy 2.0, batteries) has often moved the market fundamentally.

The data shows PHES projects require government co-investment, concessional finance, or both to reach viability. Kidston's successful $775M financing required NAIF debt ($610M), ARENA grant ($47M), strategic equity from J-POWER, and government co-funding of transmission ($147M) — a financing complexity that has proven unrepeatable for other private proponents.

## The Evidence Base

This profile draws on 486 records spanning 2012-2024 across 15 PHES projects, with 62 records (12.8%) carrying temporal warnings for pre-2021 insights on fast-moving technology conditions. The dataset includes three deep case studies: Kidston (successful, 250MW), Shoalhaven expansion (cancelled by Origin), and Cultana seawater PHES (cancelled by EnergyAustralia). Recent records (2022-2024) provide high-confidence insights on current construction challenges, while earlier records (2016-2021) reveal persistent patterns in market and regulatory failures.

Coverage is strongest on design and financing dimensions (176 and 85 adverse records respectively), with substantial data on siting challenges (120 records) and grid connection complexities (59 records). The temporal distribution shows consistent delivery challenges across the full period, with recent projects facing intensified cost pressures rather than resolved implementation barriers.

## Where Things Go Wrong

### Commercial Viability Crisis

**PHES faces a fundamental commercial crisis in the Australian market.** The evidence shows systematic cost escalation that destroys project business cases between feasibility and EPC tender. "The experience with the Project is that the estimated capital cost significantly increased as it moved from feasibility, to development and ultimately construction stage," reports Kidston. This pattern repeats across projects: EnergyAustralia's Cultana project hit $790.5M against earlier estimates, while Origin's detailed feasibility for Shoalhaven produced EPC bids of $570-630M that were "materially higher" than pre-feasibility estimates.

The root causes are structural, not project-specific. **Australia has no recent PHES construction experience** — "As the Project is the first of its kind constructed in Australia in 40 years, the only observable comparisons are with other PSH projects under development." This forces contractors to apply massive risk premiums. The limited pool of capable civil contractors creates an oligopoly pricing effect, with analysis showing "an increase in the cost of PHES following detailed and site-specific reviews has been seen across multiple proposed projects in Australia."

Market revenues compound the problem. Snowy 2.0's emergence as a 2,000MW competitor fundamentally altered the business case for smaller projects: "The addition of Snowy 2.0 has a large impact on the forecast revenues reducing revenues to ~$110,000 per MW p.a. immediately after Snowy 2.0 comes on-line" for the Shoalhaven project. Battery cost declines create further pressure: "BESS cost and capability are rapidly developing and eroding the market opportunity for medium duration PHES."

### Underground Civil Works Complexity

**Geological uncertainty dominates technical risk and drives the highest cost contingencies.** Recent evidence from Kidston's construction demonstrates the severity: "During excavation of the tailrace tunnels, high-pressure water ingress was encountered, with water pressures up to 180m head in CA02 and up to 120m head in the tailrace tunnels, with inflows exceeding 2000L/minute from single standpipe holes." The project team required modified drill-and-blast sequences, standpipes, probing, and pressure grouting — all unplanned costs and schedule impacts.

The 2022 water ingress event at Kidston forced a complete realignment of the Main Access Tunnel: "Following the September 2022 water ingress event, the MAT alignment was redesigned in November 2022. A new Y-junction was introduced and a revised MAT2 alignment extended the spiral by one additional loop." This wasn't an isolated incident — the evidence shows geological surprises are systematic, not exceptional.

Contractor capability adds another layer of complexity. "Raisebore shaft reaming operations require frequent halting to remove cuttings from the shaft base; failure to monitor and remove cuttings creates a risk of shaft choking and uncontrolled release of material into the underground works." The precision required for deep underground construction in Australia's geological conditions strains available contractor expertise.

### Regulatory and Approvals Labyrinth

**PHES faces the most complex approvals pathway of any renewable technology.** The Cultana project's experience illustrates the challenge: "More than 12 months passed before the submission requirements for the Department of Environment and Energy (DoEE) Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation (EPBC) Act assessment were advised. This pushed the required time frame to achieve Development Approval well beyond the FID target date."

Multiple agencies operate without coordination. "As the project was planned to be predominantly located on land owned by the DoD, but would also require infrastructure to cross State Crown land and into State marine waters (the Spencer Gulf), early consultation was carried out" — but determining which approvals applied took over a year. NSW is no better: "NSW Department of Planning required a full Development Approval (including Environmental Impact Statement and public notification) for the Geotechnical Investigation Works" — even minor site investigations require full EIS processes.

Water licensing adds further complexity. Kidston required an "18-month sampling programme of the Copperfield River to establish background water chemistry before the regulatory body would set final water discharge criteria." Environmental offset requirements create additional bottlenecks: "securing suitable offset properties proved difficult and time-consuming due to landholder constraints and competing renewable energy development interest in the region."

### Financing and Offtake Impossibility

**Traditional project finance is structurally unsuited to PHES characteristics.** The fundamental problem is asset life versus investor horizons: "One of the key challenges is PHES financing, primarily due to the high capital investment versus low operating costs and long service lifetime of the plant (typically 60 to 90 years). Assets of this nature may be better financed and owned by the State or Federal Government."

Revenue forecasting uncertainty makes debt sizing impossible. "Revenue forecasting for PHES in the NEM proved highly uncertain due to rapid renewable penetration growth, proposed interconnectors, falling battery storage costs, volatile wholesale prices, and changing energy policy." Standard revenue models exclude critical revenue streams: "FCAS revenue was explicitly excluded from the financial model due to regulatory uncertainty."

**Offtake negotiations span multiple years and often fail.** Kidston's process demonstrates the timeline: "The offtake process for the Project ran at various times from 2017 to late 2019, following which Genex sought to conclude negotiations with EnergyAustralia for the binding ESSA, which was signed in late March 2020." That's three years from start to binding agreement, assuming success.

The structural mismatch between private investment horizons (20-30 years) and PHES asset life (60-90 years) cannot be resolved through contract structures alone. Kidston succeeded only through unprecedented government support: $610M NAIF debt, $47M ARENA grant, $147M government transmission co-funding, plus strategic equity. No subsequent project has replicated this financing alchemy.

## Failure Mode Deep-Dives

### Commercial & Market Failures (29% of adverse records)

Commercial failure is the leading cause of PHES project abandonment, accounting for 46% of severe outcomes. The pattern is consistent: projects advance through feasibility only to discover their business case has evaporated.

**Capital Cost Escalation**: EnergyAustralia's experience at Cultana demonstrates the scale of the problem. "The tendered prices cannot be provided for confidentiality reasons. The project teams all-inclusive estimated final cost for the facility based on the reference design and EPC tenderers pricing, including contingency and owners' expenses was $790.5 million, equivalent to $3.5 million per MW." This was approximately 50% above initial estimates and well above global benchmarks of ~$2M/MW.

The root cause isn't poor estimating — it's market structure. "Australia lacked a deep domestic hydro power industry capability at the time of the project, with expertise concentrated in Tasmanian and NSW state-owned generators. This created a thin domestic contractor market and contributed to higher-than-expected EPC prices as new entrants applied larger risk premiums."

**Revenue Compression**: Snowy 2.0's announcement fundamentally altered the competitive landscape. Analysis for Shoalhaven showed "The development of Snowy 2.0 (2,000MW) was identified as a major commercial risk, with modelling showing it would reduce forecast SPHS expansion revenues from ~$165,000/MW p.a. to ~$110,000/MW p.a. immediately upon commissioning." When 2,000MW of government-backed storage enters the same market as private projects targeting 200-400MW, the economics become impossible.

**Market Design Incompatibility**: PHES provides multiple services — energy arbitrage, inertia, system strength, black start — but only energy arbitrage has a clear price signal. "There is no current clear price signal or payment stream for these grid stability activities in the NEM. Should the provision of this service be recognised through the development of a price signal, this would provide an additional value stream." Without capacity markets or system service payments, revenue streams remain speculative.

### Regulatory & Approvals Barriers (19% of adverse records) 

Regulatory complexity creates approval timelines that interact destructively with commercial uncertainty. Projects face simultaneous State and Commonwealth processes with no coordination mechanism.

**Multi-Agency Coordination Failure**: The Cultana project exemplifies the problem: "Delays in determining the development approval pathway between State and Commonwealth agencies for the DoD site meant the EPBC Act submission requirements were not advised until September 2019 — more than 12 months after initial consultation commenced in July 2018." This 12-month delay to even understand what approvals were required pushed the project beyond its FID timeline.

**Environmental Assessment Complexity**: Even preliminary site investigations trigger full EIS requirements. "NSW Department of Planning required a full Development Approval (including Environmental Impact Statement and public notification) for the Geotechnical Investigation Works — relatively minor borehole drilling activities." When exploring a site requires the same approvals process as building the project, investigation costs and timelines become prohibitive.

**Water and Discharge Licensing**: Kidston's experience shows the depth of regulatory requirements: "An 18-month sampling programme of the Copperfield River was undertaken to establish background water chemistry. This sampling has been undertaken and the Project awaits final water discharge criteria." Projects can't proceed to detailed design without knowing discharge conditions, but discharge conditions can't be set without extensive baseline monitoring.

**Land Tenure Complexity**: Defence land adds particular complexity. Despite positive early negotiations, "DoD confirmed in August 2020 that a land lease was unable to be provided. The change in stance for the DoD came about due to the direction in the 2020 Defence Strategic Update." Two years of development work became worthless due to policy changes beyond the proponent's control.

### Execution & Logistics Challenges (18% of adverse records)

Underground construction dominates execution risk, with geological uncertainty creating systematic cost and schedule overruns.

**Geological Surprises**: The pattern is universal across projects. Kidston encountered "high-pressure water ingress with water pressures up to 180m head and inflows exceeding 2000L/minute from single standpipe holes." Cultana discovered "a previously unmapped fault under the planned lower reservoir location at approximately 18.5m depth. This required the lower reservoir to be relocated approximately 200m." Shoalhaven found "ground conditions in the vicinity of the location of the reference design power house cavern are more fractured than expected at the cavern depth."

**Contractor Capability Gaps**: The civil construction market struggles with PHES requirements. "The availability and cost of civil EPC contractors and materials was adversely affected by a concurrent peak in major infrastructure projects across Australia, reducing the competitive pool and driving up costs and construction durations."

**Remote Site Logistics**: Kidston demonstrates the compounding effects of remoteness. Transportation of 120-tonne turbines required "Self-Propelled Modular Trailers (SPMTs) for underground segments. The route involved a 6m wide, 6m high tunnel with a 14% grade and 60m radius curves." Transport for NSW had never approved SPMTs on public roads, leaving equipment delivery in regulatory limbo.

**Construction Sequencing Complexity**: Underground works interact with surface construction in complex ways. "Installation of GO4 requires full station outages to interconnect the new machine with existing penstocks and tailbays; these outages must be carefully planned to avoid impacting NEM system security or market opportunities."

### Technical Underperformance (12% of adverse records)

Technical risk concentrates in novel concepts and high-head applications where equipment is at the limits of commercial availability.

**Equipment Availability Constraints**: High-head projects face supply chain concentration. "There is considerable technical risk associated with the high head for the potential Poatina project. It is at the upper limit of existing multi-stage reversible pump/turbines for which there are limited suppliers." When only 2-3 global suppliers can manufacture suitable equipment, pricing power and delivery risk both increase.

**Seawater Corrosion**: Direct seawater PHES carries systematic technical penalties. "Most suppliers suggested the repairs and maintenance requirements, including downtime for routine maintenance, would be twice that as compared with a freshwater PHES." The Cultana project abandoned direct seawater after supplier feedback indicated doubled maintenance costs and "uncertain component lifetimes."

**System Integration Complexity**: PHES requires coordination across multiple systems — civil, mechanical, electrical, control — where interface risks multiply. Underground powerhouses add particular complexity due to access constraints for maintenance and the irreversibility of certain design decisions.

## What Has Changed Over Time

The risk profile has intensified rather than improved across the study period. **Three negative trends dominate:**

**Capital Cost Escalation**: Recent projects face higher costs than earlier estimates suggested. Kidston's final cost of $775M represented significant escalation from feasibility estimates, driven by "five years of CPI escalation, and COVID-19 pandemic pricing pressures." This sets a challenging benchmark for future projects.

**Market Competitive Pressure**: The emergence of Snowy 2.0 and rapid battery cost declines fundamentally altered the competitive landscape after 2018. Earlier studies assumed PHES would face limited competition, but current projects must compete against government-backed large-scale storage and rapidly improving short-duration alternatives.

**Regulatory Complexity Increase**: Environmental requirements have strengthened rather than streamlined. EPBC offset requirements are more stringent, water licensing more complex, and the interaction between State and Commonwealth processes less coordinated than in earlier periods.

**One positive trend emerges**: Understanding of geological risk has improved through bitter experience. Recent projects like Kidston employ sophisticated water management (standpipes, probing, grouting) from the outset rather than treating geological conditions as a late-stage surprise. Construction methodology has evolved to manage known risks more effectively.

However, this improvement in execution capability has been overwhelmed by worsening commercial and regulatory conditions. The evidence suggests PHES delivery risk has increased over the study period despite accumulated technical learning.

## Due Diligence Checklist

### Commercial Viability Gates
- **Capital cost validation**: Demand competitive EPC tenders before FID. If the proponent is working from feasibility estimates without market-tested pricing, budget for 50%+ escalation and question project readiness.
- **Competing storage analysis**: How does the project's revenue case change if Snowy 2.0 operates at full capacity and battery costs decline 10% annually? If project economics collapse under these scenarios, the business case is inadequately stress-tested.
- **Revenue stream specificity**: What percentage of projected revenue comes from unpriced services (inertia, system strength, capacity)? If more than 20%, the project is speculating on market reform that may not occur.

### Geological and Technical Risk Assessment  
- **Site investigation completeness**: Has the proponent completed deep borehole investigation at all major structure locations (powerhouse, shafts, reservoirs)? Desktop studies and shallow investigation are insufficient for investment decisions.
- **Water management strategy**: For underground projects, what is the specific methodology for managing high-pressure water ingress? If the answer is generic ("standard dewatering"), probe deeper — recent projects require standpipes, pressure grouting, and modified blasting sequences.
- **Equipment supplier engagement**: Has the proponent confirmed equipment availability and pricing with turbine OEMs? For high-head projects (>600m), verify that multiple suppliers can bid and that lead times are realistic.

### Regulatory Pathway Validation
- **Approval timeline verification**: Cross-check the proponent's approvals timeline against recent comparable projects. If they claim faster approvals than Kidston (3+ years) or Cultana (2+ years before cancellation), demand specific justification.
- **Environmental offset strategy**: For projects triggering EPBC requirements, is there a signed offset agreement or confirmed available offset land? Conceptual offset strategies are a major schedule risk.
- **Water licensing certainty**: For projects requiring water discharge or transfer, are the regulatory criteria confirmed in writing? An 18-month baseline monitoring requirement cannot be accelerated through commercial pressure.

### Financing Structure Adequacy
- **Government co-investment**: Does the financing plan include government debt (NAIF), grants (ARENA), or transmission co-funding? If the project assumes pure private finance, compare against successful precedents — only Kidston has achieved FID, and it required all three forms of government support.
- **Offtake counterparty strength**: Who is the proposed offtaker and what is their retail book size? Small retailers cannot absorb the output of large PHES projects; major gentailers have pricing power that may crush project returns.
- **Development timeline realism**: How long from current stage to first revenue? If less than 5 years total, scrutinize each workstream for feasibility — PHES projects that claim accelerated timelines typically encounter delays that destroy financing arrangements.

The evidence shows PHES requires patient capital, government support, and exceptional execution capability. Projects that cannot demonstrate all three elements are unlikely to reach commercial operation in the current Australian market environment.
