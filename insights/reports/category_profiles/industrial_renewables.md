# Industrial Renewables — Delivery Risk Profile

## Executive Summary

Industrial renewables projects face fundamentally different risks than grid-scale renewables. This isn't just about connecting solar panels or heat pumps to industrial facilities — it's about retrofitting complex thermal systems into live production environments where a poorly timed shutdown costs hundreds of thousands of dollars and where a failed heat recovery project can double the size of a wastewater treatment plant.

Three failure modes dominate the risk profile: **Commercial & market** challenges (30% of adverse outcomes) driven by unfavorable electricity-to-gas price ratios and inadequate electrical infrastructure; **Technical underperformance** (20%) concentrated in heat pump integration, electrode degradation, and waste heat recovery shortfalls; and **Coordination & stakeholders** issues (15%) spanning everything from university partners abandoning software deliverables to councils blocking EV charger deployments.

The data spans 144 projects from 2012-2025, with 89% of records from 2019 onward. Recent projects show industrial renewables have crossed into commercial viability in specific applications — heat pumps in meatworks, thermal storage for grid arbitrage, electrochemical ironmaking — but remain highly sensitive to site-specific constraints and require sophisticated project management to navigate live production environments.

## The Evidence Base

The dataset covers 1233 adverse outcomes across 144 projects, with strong representation of recent work (461 records from 2022-2024, 345 from 2025+). Only 64 records (5.2%) carry temporal warnings about outdated technology costs or capabilities, indicating most findings reflect current conditions.

Coverage spans the full technology spectrum from heat pumps and waste heat recovery through to cutting-edge electrochemical ironmaking and thermal energy storage. The project portfolio ranges from $2M heat pump demonstrations to $100M+ greenfield industrial decarbonisation facilities, providing insight across the commercial scale spectrum.

The data quality is particularly strong for recent ARENA flagship projects like the MGA Thermal demonstration, Alcoa's electric calcination pilot, and Fortescue's direct electrochemical reduction program, where detailed lessons-learned reports capture both technical failures and project management insights not typically available in academic literature.

## Where Things Go Wrong

### Commercial & Market Barriers Dominate (30% of Failures)

Industrial renewables projects fail commercially more often than technically. The fundamental barrier is simple: at current Australian energy prices, electricity costs 2-4× more than gas per GJ, meaning electrification via heat pumps needs COP of 3-4 just to break even on operating costs. 

"At the current gas:electricity price ratio, electrification projects have a relatively high simple payback period," the G. & K. O'Connor study found, with heat pump paybacks of 15-21 years before certificates. The Brown Family Wine Group analysis was blunt: even with a COP of 3-4, heat pump projects had paybacks "quite long at over 10 years" when electricity and gas costs were roughly equal at $27-28/GJ.

The problem compounds when projects require electrical infrastructure upgrades. Multiple projects encountered what amounts to a double capital hit: the technology itself plus the electrical infrastructure to power it. At the Pakenham abattoir, "proposed electrification projects requiring a minimum of 600 kVA were found to be infeasible on existing electrical infrastructure" with "no additional electrical capacity on the abattoir MSB without load management or upgrade." The Quantem study was unequivocal: "None of the assessed sites currently have proper infrastructure to support electrification."

Grid connection complexity adds another layer. The Western Power data shows connection enquiries surged following state decarbonisation commitments, with only 5% reaching Access Offer before recent reforms. For larger projects, Ausnet discussions revealed "a significant risk that all three NMIs at the site, being on a single HV feeder, could be treated by AEMO as a single connection point exceeding 5 MVA, triggering onerous compliance requirements, timelines of approximately 2 years for approval, and estimated connection costs of around $600,000."

### Technical Underperformance in Complex Integration (20% of Failures)

Technical failures in industrial renewables cluster around three themes: electrode degradation in electrochemical processes, heat pump integration challenges, and waste heat recovery shortfalls.

The electrochemical ironmaking projects provide the clearest view of technical complexity. Fortescue's DER program encountered recurring electrode delamination where "the carbon layer delaminated" due to "chemical incompatibility between the polymer and the 50% aqueous NaOH electrolyte, and the active temperature of the cell being above the Glass Transition Temperature (Tg) of the original Ampreg-31 epoxy." The solution — fully encapsulated electrodes — required fundamental design changes.

Heat pump integration proved more complex than anticipated across multiple projects. At Hardwick Meatworks, "During the commissioning period, various issues were rectified or refined: Air lock in the oil cooling lines rectified. Heating of the compressor oil sump rectified. Higher pressure-drops across oil separator than planned. Vibrations on the heat pump skid were rectified." The project succeeded, but the commissioning phase revealed that industrial heat pump deployment isn't plug-and-play.

Waste heat recovery consistently underperformed expectations. The G. & K. O'Connor study found that "A heat recovery loop between the render plant vacuum condenser and the abattoir hot water tanks... achieved average pre-heated water temperatures of only 34°C rather than the design value of 55°C." This forced a complete technology pivot from desuperheaters to ammonia heat pumps. The Real Pet Foods analysis was more direct: "Energy audits overestimated waste heat available for a heat pump application because audits assumed equipment operates at maximum capacity continuously, whereas in reality heat sources were intermittent and lower grade on average."

### Coordination Failures Across Organizational Boundaries (15% of Failures)

Industrial renewables projects routinely fail due to coordination breakdowns that would be minor issues in other contexts but become fatal in complex multi-party implementations.

The most dramatic example comes from the PCM thermal energy storage project, where "UniSA, the key software development partner responsible for developing and deploying the ACFA control software, withdrew from the project in September 2022 after the Commercial Research Agreement expired with significant deliverables incomplete." The software was left "in an academic, non-commercial state that required a full rewrite to become a viable product." This killed the commercialisation pathway despite successful hardware demonstration across three sites.

University partnerships emerged as a systematic coordination risk. The project found that "Software developed by academic teams for demonstration projects is frequently not production-ready and should not be assumed to be commercially deployable without independent technical assessment." Academic teams "lack the commercial software engineering experience required to produce production-grade code, and CRAs with universities should explicitly require completion of deliverables, not just best-efforts participation."

Council engagement created unexpected barriers for street-level infrastructure. The EV charger project discovered that "A high proportion of initially selected sites were rejected at various stages of the 10–15 step sequential approval process" and that "Traffic committees in NSW meet bi-monthly and missing one meeting adds up to 4 months of delay." The team learned to "select 2–4 times as many candidate sites as you plan to install" to absorb rejection rates.

Even within organizations, coordination challenges emerged. The Alcoa ECP project noted that "Key project roles were staffed with part-time resources who had competing demands on their time... this led to delays in design deliverables, delayed scheduling of critical workshops (including HAZOPs), and delayed procurement activities."

## Failure Mode Deep-Dives

### Commercial & Market Failure: The Electricity Price Trap

Commercial failure in industrial renewables follows a predictable pattern: compelling environmental outcomes undermined by adverse energy price ratios and unanticipated infrastructure costs.

The mechanism starts with a basic physics problem. Industrial process heat typically requires 80-120°C delivery temperatures. Heat pumps can achieve this, but COP degrades as lift temperature increases. Even with COP of 3-4, the 2-4× electricity price premium over gas makes operating costs break-even at best. The Brown Family Wine Group study captured this precisely: "The electricity cost and gas cost per GJ are roughly the same at this site at around $27 to $28/GJ. Even with a COP of 3 to 4, the payback periods for heat pumps is quite long at over 10 years."

The problem compounds when electrical infrastructure isn't adequate. Multiple projects discovered that electrification isn't just buying different equipment — it's rebuilding the electrical systems. At Hardwick Meatworks, "The site's prior 2 MVA LV supply was a binding power constraint that prevented addition of the 250 kW heat pump electrical load. Upgrading to a 4 MVA HV supply at 22 kV from the Powercor network was a prerequisite for the heat pump project."

Grid connection adds a third cost layer. The data shows connection processes have materially lengthened as decarbonisation demand surges. Energy Queensland's major project volumes "grew from approximately 20 projects in 2021 to approximately 110 projects by 2025," a five-fold increase straining resources. Connection timeframes of "18 months to 48 months from enquiry to commissioning" are now routine for projects above 1 MVA.

The financial result is often devastating. The Quantem study found direct electrification options producing "negative cost savings (i.e. cost increases) at all sites, with NPVs ranging from -$4M to -$25M." Even the most promising option — steam replacement with hot water and heat pumps — delivered an NPV of -$26.6M with a 110-year payback.

Yet success cases exist where economics align. The DON Smallgoods study found that refrigeration heat recovery "could reduce natural gas use on the site by up to 24%" where "The quantity of thermal heat rejected by the refrigeration systems at Don Smallgoods Castlemaine is directly comparable to the amount of thermal heat generation by the site steam systems." The key was matching waste heat supply to thermal demand at appropriate temperatures without requiring electrical infrastructure upgrades.

### Technical Underperformance: When Innovation Meets Operating Reality

Technical underperformance in industrial renewables rarely results from fundamental technology failure. Instead, it emerges from the collision between laboratory-optimized technology and the messy reality of continuous industrial operations.

Electrochemical processes provide the clearest examples. The DER ironmaking program achieved world-class laboratory results but encountered systematic materials compatibility issues at operating scale. "Composite carbon electrodes in the DER electrolyser experienced recurring delamination of the carbon layer from the steel backing plate." Investigation revealed two failure mechanisms: "chemical incompatibility between the polymer binder and the 50% aqueous NaOH electrolyte, and operating temperatures exceeding the glass transition temperature (Tg) of the polymer."

The solution required fundamental design changes. "Fully encapsulated carbon–titanium composite electrodes demonstrated strong resistance to 50 wt.% NaOH at 100 °C, maintaining electrochemical activity for up to 136 hours — substantially longer than earlier non-encapsulated specimens." But reaching this solution consumed significant project time and resources that could have been avoided with more thorough materials screening upfront.

Heat recovery systems consistently delivered lower performance than design assumptions. The pattern repeated across projects: energy audits assumed peak conditions, but reality delivered intermittent, lower-grade heat sources. At Real Pet Foods, "refrigeration plant rejected heat at 25°C rather than the 35°C maximum used in audit calculations." The G. & K. O'Connor project found waste heat averaging 34°C instead of the design 55°C, forcing complete technology reconsideration.

Integration challenges proved particularly costly because they emerge late in project lifecycles. The MGA thermal storage project discovered that "Heat Transfer: TES and HRSGs must be located in close proximity to the generating units to minimise steam travel distance and heat loss" — a thermodynamic constraint that forced complete site layout redesign and ultimately required "demolition of all four boilers to free up the required footprint."

The common thread is inadequate characterization of actual operating conditions during early project phases. Desktop studies and peak-condition assumptions systematically overstate performance potential, creating business cases that appear attractive but cannot be delivered in practice.

### Coordination & Stakeholders: The Multi-Party Execution Challenge

Coordination failures in industrial renewables stem from the technology's inherent requirement to work across organizational and operational boundaries. Unlike utility-scale renewables that operate on greenfield sites with single owners, industrial renewables must integrate into live production environments involving multiple stakeholders with different priorities and capabilities.

University partnerships represent the highest-risk coordination relationship. The PCM thermal energy storage project provides the template for how this fails. "UniSA management treated the Commercial Research Agreement (CRA) as an effort-based obligation rather than an outcomes-based deliverable contract." When "the CRA term expired in March 2022, key project resources were no longer employed by the university" due to COVID-era HR policies. The result: "The ACFA software was left in an academic, non-commercial state that required a full rewrite to become a viable product."

The fundamental issue is mismatched expectations. Universities optimize for research publications; industrial projects require deployable products. "Academic research teams with domain expertise do not automatically possess commercial software engineering capability," yet contracts often assume this translation will happen automatically. The solution requires explicit specification: "When engaging a university partner to develop deployable control software, explicitly require commercial software engineering resources and verify this capability before contract execution."

Local government coordination introduces different challenges. The EV charger deployment found that "Council community engagement and traffic committee approval processes introduced structured, multi-week delays at each site" with "traffic committees in NSW meet bi-monthly and missing one meeting adds up to 4 months of delay per site." The team learned to "approach significantly more councils than required" because "only 8 of 12 approached agreed to participate."

Industrial site coordination requires navigating production constraints that don't exist in greenfield development. The PCM installation at Pernod Ricard discovered that "The Fridge 9 plant operates year-round, providing process cooling for packing areas, barrel storage and air conditioning for the auditorium, this meant the shutting down of Fridge 9 for any extended period of time was not possible." The solution was "organising several small shutdowns to install individual components" — possible, but adding complexity and cost.

Skills shortages compound coordination challenges. The thermal energy storage project found that "The most unforeseen challenge to the project from a technical perspective was the lack of suitably trained technicians to carryout the post commissioning service and maintenance." For novel technologies, "technology providers should bring service and maintenance capability in-house as a standard part of the commercial model rather than relying on third-party service providers."

## What Has Changed Over Time

The industrial renewables risk profile has evolved substantially as the sector matured from early demonstrations to commercial deployment, though fundamental challenges persist.

**Grid connection processes have become both more systematic and more constrained.** The Western Power data shows connection enquiry success rates improving from 5% to 23% after implementing the Critical Projects Framework, but Energy Queensland's project volumes multiplying five-fold from 2021-2025 indicates demand has outpaced capacity expansion. "Current state ~23% of enquiries will reach access offer" represents meaningful progress, but "Typical Timeframes: vary based on project, at the larger end: 18mth to 48mth" shows the system remains stressed.

**Technology readiness has advanced significantly for specific applications.** Heat pumps in meatworks and food processing now have multiple reference sites. The Hardwick Meatworks project "demonstrated that a low-technology ammonia-loop heat pump configuration was viable and appropriate for a meatworks environment, reducing gas usage by approximately 61%." Thermal energy storage has moved from concept to demonstration scale: "MGA Thermal successfully designed, constructed, commissioned and operated a 5 MWh electrically heated thermal energy storage (eTES) demonstration plant at Tomago, NSW."

**Electrochemical ironmaking has progressed from laboratory curiosity to pilot-scale planning.** Fortescue's DER program advanced from materials development to "a Financial Investment Decision (FID) by the Fortescue Executive Board, scheduled for March 2025" for a 150 t/year pilot with "first green iron product targeted for mid-2027." Multiple competing technologies are racing toward commercialisation: "Boston Metal, the leading company working in the space, announced in early 2025 the successful commissioning of an industrial-scale pilot cell."

**Commercial frameworks remain the primary limiting factor.** The fundamental electricity-gas price ratio that undermines most industrial electrification business cases hasn't materially changed. Projects continue to encounter the same infrastructure upgrade requirements and grid connection complexities. However, successful models are emerging where waste heat recovery, thermal storage arbitrage, or process efficiency improvements can overcome the price headwinds.

**Risk management sophistication has increased.** Recent projects show better understanding of coordination challenges, with more sophisticated contracting for university partnerships and better stakeholder management. The NeoSmelt consortium's development of "a multi-layered governance structure comprising an Operating Committee, plus Technical and IP Subcommittees, plus multiple working groups" represents best practice for complex multi-party projects.

## Due Diligence Checklist

### Commercial Viability Screening
- **Energy price analysis**: Request detailed electricity vs gas cost modeling including demand charges and peak rates. If electricity exceeds 2× gas cost per GJ, heat pumps need COP ≥4 for operating cost neutrality. Challenge any COP assumptions above 3.5 for industrial applications above 80°C.
- **Electrical infrastructure assessment**: Require a qualified electrical engineer to assess existing capacity before scoping any electrification above 200 kW. If upgrades exceed 20% of technology cost, red-flag the project economics and consider deferral until planned electrical renewals.
- **Grid connection pathway**: For projects above 1 MVA, initiate DNSP discussions immediately and assume 18-48 month approval timelines. If connection costs exceed $200k or require new transmission, reassess project viability.

### Technical Risk Evaluation
- **Reference site performance**: Demand operational data from at least two reference sites with similar process conditions. Laboratory or pilot data alone is insufficient for commercial-scale commitment. If reference sites don't exist, budget for extended commissioning and performance tuning.
- **Waste heat characterization**: Require continuous temperature and flow monitoring for minimum 3 months across seasonal and production variations. Energy audit estimates typically overstate by 30-50%. If waste heat recovery is >25% of project value, install monitoring before finalizing technology selection.
- **Integration complexity**: Map all existing control systems, safety systems, and process interdependencies. If >3 different PLC/SCADA systems must interface, flag as high coordination risk and budget additional systems integration time and cost.

### Coordination & Stakeholder Management
- **University partnerships**: If software development is required, verify commercial software engineering capability and insist on outcomes-based contracts with completion obligations. Avoid effort-based CRAs for critical commercial deliverables. Budget for commercial software rewrite if the deliverable is mission-critical.
- **Local government approvals**: For street-level infrastructure (EV charging, distribution networks), engage councils 6-9 months before planned deployment and submit to multiple councils assuming 50-70% rejection rates. Budget for traffic committee cycles and community consultation timelines.
- **Production continuity planning**: For retrofits in operating facilities, confirm maintenance windows and shutdown tolerances upfront. If <4-hour shutdown windows are available, consider modular installation approaches or temporary redundancy. Never assume extended shutdowns are feasible without explicit production approval.

### Commercial Due Diligence Red Flags
- **Payback >10 years without policy support**: Projects with paybacks exceeding 10 years are vulnerable to management changes, competing capital priorities, and policy shifts. Seek co-funding or defer until economics improve.
- **Single point of failure dependencies**: If project viability depends on a single technology vendor, regulatory approval, or offtake agreement, red-flag as high execution risk. Develop explicit contingency pathways before commitment.
- **First-of-a-kind technology without staged deployment**: For novel technologies, insist on pilot-scale demonstration before commercial commitment. The MVR evaporation project's cost escalation from $35M to $79M demonstrates the risk of skipping staged validation.

### Success Indicators to Look For
- **Demonstrated operational flexibility**: Projects that can accommodate production schedule variations, partial performance, or phased implementation are more likely to succeed in industrial environments.
- **Integrated business case**: Projects that deliver multiple benefits (energy cost reduction, demand charge management, process improvement, emissions reduction) are more resilient to individual assumption failures.
- **Experienced industrial integration team**: Teams with demonstrated industrial retrofit experience, particularly in similar process environments, significantly reduce execution risk compared to greenfield renewable developers entering industrial markets.
