# Industrial Renewables — Delivery Risk Profile

## Executive Summary

Industrial renewables projects face a complex risk landscape dominated by commercial viability challenges and technical performance issues. Portfolio managers should expect approximately 30% of adverse events to stem from commercial and market factors, often driven by electricity cost sensitivity and unproven business cases. Technical underperformance affects 20% of adverse events, frequently involving novel electrochemical processes, integration challenges, and materials compatibility issues. The sector shows significant learning from early ARENA projects, with newer projects (2022+) demonstrating improved risk management, though fundamental challenges around scale-up, feedstock variability, and commercial readiness persist.

## Coverage and Data Quality

This profile draws from 1,233 records across 144 projects spanning 2012-2024. The dataset is well-distributed temporally, with 37% of records from 2022-2024 representing current conditions. Only 5.2% of records carry temporal warnings, primarily related to cost curves and technology capability from pre-2021 sources. The data encompasses diverse technologies including electrochemical processes (DER, electrolysers), thermal systems (heat pumps, furnaces), and integration projects. Coverage is strongest in design, financing, and procurement dimensions, with good representation across failure modes.

## Risk Landscape by Delivery Dimension

**Design emerges as the highest-risk dimension** (391 adverse records), with technical underperformance (32%) and commercial factors (24%) dominating. Design failures often involve materials selection for harsh operating environments (hot caustic, high-temperature), feedstock compatibility issues, and inadequate process integration planning. The prevalence of data gaps (15%) indicates insufficient baseline characterisation before design freeze.

**Financing presents acute commercial challenges** (190 records, 77% commercial failures). Industrial renewables projects consistently struggle with business case validation, particularly around electricity cost sensitivity. Multiple heat pump and electrochemical projects show negative NPVs despite technical feasibility, with payback periods exceeding 10-20 years common. The BALIO process exemplifies this - technically successful but requiring electricity below $66/MWh for viability.

**Procurement risks cluster around execution challenges** (171 records, 25% execution, 23% technical). Novel equipment sourcing presents systematic challenges - almost all log furnace suppliers are European, creating supply chain and support risks for Australian operators. First-of-a-kind equipment procurement shows recurring issues with materials compatibility, interface integration, and supplier experience gaps.

**Grid connection faces regulatory bottlenecks** (92 records, 38% regulatory). Connection processes show structural dysfunction - Western Power's 2022 situation where only 5% of enquiries reached Access Offer exemplifies system-level failure. However, recent reforms demonstrate improvement potential, with Energy Queensland achieving 23% success rates through structured readiness frameworks.

**Software and controls integration** (114 records) shows elevated data measurement challenges (25%) and unvalidated integration issues (17%). Industrial control system integration with existing PLCs and SCADA systems consistently underestimated in project planning, particularly for heat pump retrofits requiring integration with multiple legacy systems.

## Failure Mode Deep-Dive

**Commercial & Market (30% of adverse events, 32% severity escalation)**
Commercial failures manifest as electricity cost sensitivity overwhelming project economics, even for technically successful processes. The DER project achieved technical milestones but faces commercialisation barriers due to CAPEX estimates exceeding targets by 2×. Heat pump projects at BFWG and Castlemaine achieved technical feasibility but delivered negative NPVs due to energy price parity between gas and electricity. These failures aren't technical - they reflect insufficient early-stage commercial validation and failure to secure appropriate energy pricing structures before technology development.

**Technical Underperformance (20% of adverse events, 18% severity escalation)**
Technical failures centre on materials compatibility in harsh industrial environments and feedstock variability. The DER project demonstrates the pattern: electrode materials delaminated in hot caustic conditions, requiring extensive materials re-qualification. Iron ore feedstock variability between mines and batches created reproducibility challenges that undermined process optimisation. Unlike early ARENA projects where technical failures often reflected immature technologies, current failures indicate insufficient materials testing and feedstock characterisation before scale-up commitments.

**Coordination & Stakeholders (15% of adverse events, 23% severity escalation)**
Coordination failures appear in two distinct patterns: multi-party consortium management and regulatory/grid interface issues. The NeoSmelt joint venture exemplifies successful coordination through structured governance, while grid connection projects suffer from misaligned expectations and process dysfunction. The Western Power queue of 236 projects with 68% "zombies" illustrates coordination failure at system level, though the subsequent MCCP reforms demonstrate that structured coordination frameworks can dramatically improve outcomes.

**Data & Measurement (12% of adverse events, 7% severity escalation)**
Data failures consistently involve inadequate baseline characterisation before design commitments. Industrial sites planning electrification routinely lack sub-metering on major energy systems, requiring costly retrofit programs that consume feasibility study budgets. The Castlemaine study required installation of 13 flow meters and 10 electrical submeters before meaningful analysis could begin. These failures are predictable and preventable through early data infrastructure investment.

## Temporal Trends

**2012-2018: Technology Focus**
Early ARENA projects concentrated on proving technical feasibility, with failures predominantly around immature technologies and basic integration challenges. Cost curves and performance assumptions from this period require updating, as flagged by temporal warnings.

**2019-2021: Scale-up Challenges**
The middle period saw increased focus on demonstration scale projects, revealing systematic challenges around materials compatibility, supply chain constraints, and commercial readiness gaps. Many projects achieved technical milestones but struggled with business case validation.

**2022-2024: Commercial Maturation**
Recent projects show improved technical risk management but persistent commercial challenges. Electricity cost sensitivity has emerged as the dominant commercial barrier, with successful technical demonstrations failing commercial viability tests. However, successful coordination frameworks (Western Power MCCP, NeoSmelt JV) demonstrate institutional learning.

The trend suggests industrial renewables technologies are approaching technical maturity but face structural commercial and regulatory barriers that require policy-level intervention rather than technology development alone.

## Key Watchpoints for Due Diligence

**1. Electricity Cost Sensitivity Analysis**
Stress-test project economics across electricity price scenarios spanning $30-100/MWh. Many technically successful projects fail commercial viability above $60-70/MWh. Secure long-term electricity pricing agreements before technology development commitments.

**2. Materials Compatibility Validation**
For any process involving high temperatures, caustic environments, or novel feedstocks, require explicit materials compatibility testing at operating conditions before design freeze. Literature reviews are insufficient - demand experimental validation with actual process materials and conditions.

**3. Feedstock Characterisation Completeness**
Where projects depend on variable industrial feedstocks (iron ore, waste streams, biomass), require comprehensive feedstock variability assessment across suppliers and seasons. Synthetic laboratory surrogates consistently under-represent real-world complexity.

**4. Grid Connection Readiness Framework**
Engage with network providers early using structured readiness criteria (land access, financing commitments, technical studies). Avoid contributing to "zombie project" queues that block capacity for viable developments.

**5. Integration and Controls Complexity**
For retrofit projects, audit existing control systems and plan explicitly for PLC/SCADA integration complexity. Budget 20-30% additional engineering effort beyond standalone equipment costs for controls integration.

**6. Supply Chain and Support Infrastructure**
For novel industrial equipment, map global supplier base and local support capabilities. European-dominated supply chains create schedule, currency, and support risks that must be explicitly managed.

**7. Commercial Readiness Validation**
Require parallel commercial and technical development tracks, not sequential. Commercial market studies, off-taker engagement, and business model validation should progress alongside technology development to avoid late-stage commercial failures of technically successful projects.
