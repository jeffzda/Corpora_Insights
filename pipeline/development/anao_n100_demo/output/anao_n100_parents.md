## Full parent definitions

### p01 — Performance Measurement and Indicator Validity Failures

Failures in which the apparatus of performance measurement — indicators, targets, baselines, and reporting — does not faithfully represent the underlying reality being managed. Membership requires that the mechanism concerns whether what is measured or reported is a valid, comparable, or meaningful signal of performance. Excludes failures of monitoring frequency or analytical follow-through, which belong elsewhere.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism turns on a defect in the design, validity, comparability, or completeness of performance measures, targets, or reported performance figures.

**Exemplar clusters:** c011, c037, c079, c107, c118, c176, c177
**Estimated population:** 5-7%

### p02 — Outcome Measurement and Evaluation Absence

Failures in which programs cannot demonstrate or attribute results because outcome measurement, evaluation infrastructure, or causal-attribution design was never established. Distinct from indicator-validity failures: here the issue is that no mechanism exists to test whether the program works, not that an existing mechanism is biased.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism concerns the absence of outcome measurement, evaluation, or attribution capacity, leaving program effectiveness unknowable.

**Exemplar clusters:** c053, c061, c072, c082, c150
**Estimated population:** 2-4%

### p03 — Survey and Self-Report Instrument Bias

Failures in which information collected from individuals is systematically distorted by the design of the collection instrument or the social conditions of collection. Membership requires that the bias arises from instrument structure, voluntariness, or respondent dependency rather than from data system defects.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that a survey, feedback, or self-reporting process produces systematically biased input because of how it is designed or administered.

**Exemplar clusters:** c009, c010, c031, c102
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p04 — Data Architecture and Integrity Defects

Failures rooted in the structure or quality of data holdings — fragmentation across systems, definitional incompatibility, missing input controls, migration gaps, or pervasive data quality problems — that prevent reliable downstream use. Excludes IT system functionality gaps that drive workarounds and access-control deficiencies, which are separate parents.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is a structural defect in how data is stored, defined, validated, migrated, or aggregated that corrupts or obscures the operational picture.

**Exemplar clusters:** c001, c002, c027, c098, c134, c152, c180
**Estimated population:** 4-6%

### p05 — IT System Functionality Gaps and Workarounds

Failures in which corporate information systems lack required functionality, force manual workarounds, fail under load, or are inconsistent across versions, generating inefficiency, error, and integrity risk. Distinct from data architecture failures (which concern stored content) and from access-control failures (which concern security).

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that IT system capability — functionality, capacity, version consistency, or testing fidelity — is inadequate for the operational task it must support.

**Exemplar clusters:** c042, c043, c047, c127, c181
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p06 — Information Security and Access Control Weaknesses

Failures in which sensitive information is exposed, exfiltrated, or accessible beyond legitimate need because access controls, audit trails, or security review processes are absent or inadequate. Includes risks amplified by digitisation of personal data.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism turns on inadequate controls over who can access, alter, or extract sensitive information, or on the absence of audit trails to detect such access.

**Exemplar clusters:** c076, c096, c124, c189
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p07 — Recordkeeping and Decision Documentation Gaps

Failures in which the rationale, evidence, or content of decisions and actions is not captured in the record, eroding accountability, organisational memory, and the capacity to review or reconstruct what occurred. Distinct from data integrity failures: the issue is documentary practice, not data systems.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that decisions, rationales, or actions are not documented in a manner that supports later accountability, review, or learning.

**Exemplar clusters:** c018, c029, c179
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p08 — Reporting Misrepresentation to Decision-Makers

Failures in which the information transmitted upward to executives, ministers, parliament, or oversight bodies is materially misleading — through false assurance, omission of material analysis, factual error, undisclosed methodology change, internal inconsistency, or reframed scope. Distinct from indicator-validity failures: here the corruption occurs in the reporting/briefing channel itself.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that information reaching decision-makers misrepresents the actual state of affairs through suppression, error, or selective framing.

**Exemplar clusters:** c005, c062, c088, c092, c094, c130
**Estimated population:** 3-5%

### p09 — Disclosure and Transparency Compliance Failures

Failures in which mandatory or expected disclosure to external stakeholders — applicants, the public, parliamentary committees, or regulated parties — is incomplete, untimely, or systematically misclassified. Distinct from internal misrepresentation: here the failure concerns external visibility.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that information owed to external stakeholders is withheld, delayed, misclassified, or only partially disclosed against an applicable transparency standard.

**Exemplar clusters:** c068, c121, c167, c186, c206
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p10 — Risk Management Framework Deficiencies

Failures in which formal risk management processes are absent, narrowly scoped, fragmented across silos, or fail to translate identified risks into action. Includes ignoring known pre-existing risks at launch and failing to use risk data to calibrate insurance or coverage.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is a defect in how risks are identified, integrated, prioritised, or acted upon within a formal risk management process.

**Exemplar clusters:** c086, c114, c156, c165, c187
**Estimated population:** 2-4%

### p11 — Governance Body and Oversight Mandate Failures

Failures in which formally constituted governance bodies, oversight functions, or assurance tracking mechanisms exist but do not perform their role — they fail to convene, lack mobilising authority, duplicate effort, prioritise nothing, or hold inherently conflicted mandates. Distinct from accountability assignment failures (which concern individuals) and from review-scope failures.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that a governance, oversight, or assurance body does not exercise its mandate effectively because of structural, capacity, or scope defects.

**Exemplar clusters:** c020, c089, c112, c126, c168
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p12 — Accountability Assignment Without Enforcement

Failures in which responsibility is formally allocated but not tied to consequences, savings are claimed without reform delivery, or roles below senior level are undefined. The mechanism is decoupling of accountability from enforceable outcome.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that responsibility for an outcome is nominally assigned but lacks consequence mechanisms, sub-level role clarity, or linkage to underlying delivery.

**Exemplar clusters:** c004, c048, c144
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p13 — Review and Assurance Scope Limitations

Failures in which formal reviews, gate assessments, recommendations follow-up, or compliance reviews exist but are scoped too narrowly, diverge from intent, or recur unresolved across cycles, allowing underlying problems to persist undetected.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that a review, audit, or assurance process is scoped or executed in a way that systematically misses the underlying issue or fails to produce corrective change.

**Exemplar clusters:** c085, c099, c105, c184
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p14 — Planning Documents Late, Absent, or Substituted

Failures in which foundational plans — strategic, operational, implementation, surveillance, quality — are unfinalised when they are needed, completed only after the operational period begins, or replaced by self-assessment templates lacking implementation detail.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the plan required to govern an activity is missing, drafted but unendorsed, finalised too late, or substituted by a non-substantive document.

**Exemplar clusters:** c007, c078, c116, c138, c146
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p15 — Strategic-to-Operational Linkage Gaps

Failures in which strategic objectives, programme objectives, or announced commitments are not coherently linked to operational selection criteria, delivery scope, or component traceability, so operations drift away from intent.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is a structural disconnection between programme objectives or commitments and the operational rules or scope that should implement them.

**Exemplar clusters:** c021, c028, c044, c110, c157
**Estimated population:** 2-4%

### p16 — Programme Design Tensions and Trade-Off Defects

Failures arising from the fundamental design configuration of a programme: dual-objective tension, design unchanged as context evolves, design that resists measurement, or design that excludes stakeholders or critical components from pilots.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the programme's core design parameters create irresolvable tensions, omit essential scope, or fail to evolve, producing predictable performance shortfalls.

**Exemplar clusters:** c015, c118, c140, c166
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p17 — Design-Phase Demand and Capacity Misestimation

Failures in which programme assumptions about demand, supply absorption, delivery capacity, or timelines are not validated against real conditions, producing material overshoot or undershoot at launch.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is unvalidated quantitative assumptions in design (about demand, supply, schedule, or delivery capacity) that fail when met with reality.

**Exemplar clusters:** c091, c131, c155, c161, c170
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p18 — Compressed Design and Implementation Windows

Failures caused by insufficient time between policy announcement, decision, or transfer and operational launch — design shortcuts, savings targets set without implementer input, due-diligence-free transitions, and pre-existing recipient commitments locking in successor entities.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the time available for design, implementation planning, or due diligence is structurally inadequate relative to the complexity of what must be delivered.

**Exemplar clusters:** c133, c141, c151, c178
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p19 — Eligibility and Decision Verification Gaps

Failures in which stated eligibility or decision criteria are not actually tested at the decision point — no verification workflow exists, third-party inputs are accepted unchecked, or undisclosed methodologies replace published criteria.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the assertion required to authorise a decision is accepted without verification against the criterion it must satisfy.

**Exemplar clusters:** c016, c036, c068
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p20 — Discretion and Assessment Process Inconsistency

Failures in which decisions across cases diverge because inputs are unstandardised, assessment processes have been progressively diluted, judgement substitutes for objective metrics, or assessor inexperience extends and degrades evaluation.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that nominally equivalent cases receive materially different treatment because the assessment process lacks standardised inputs or rigour.

**Exemplar clusters:** c017, c049, c082, c171
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p21 — Procurement Process Integrity Bypasses

Failures in which procurement rules, panel scope, competitive tendering, or probity controls are bypassed, including time-pressured direct sourcing, panel out-of-scope use, missing probity declarations, ministerial pre-notification, and failure to seek required approvals.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that mandated procurement-integrity processes (competition, probity, approval, panel rules) are circumvented in a specific transaction or programme establishment.

**Exemplar clusters:** c045, c051, c059, c077, c101
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p22 — Contract Specification and Formation Defects

Failures rooted in how contracts are written: insufficient specification of approval, control, and performance obligations; ambiguous or self-contradictory clauses; restrictive specifications excluding equivalents; failure to secure intellectual property; or rigid fee structures that cannot accommodate delivery variation.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the contract document itself, as formed, lacks the specificity, balance, or flexibility needed to govern the delivery relationship.

**Exemplar clusters:** c081, c090, c115, c128, c192, c204
**Estimated population:** 3-4%

### p23 — Contract Execution and Enforcement Failures

Failures in which contractual obligations, preconditions, security instruments, or testing requirements are not enforced through payment or delivery workflows after contract formation, including post-signature changes that escape control and serial extensions substituting for renegotiation.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that an existing contractual obligation is not operationalised, enforced, or held to its original terms during contract execution.

**Exemplar clusters:** c019, c046, c067, c111, c139, c198
**Estimated population:** 3-4%

### p24 — Dependence on Agent Without Independent Verification

Failures in which a principal accepts a contractor's, agent's, or third-party's advice, schedule, or output without independent testing, including reliance on contractor schedules whose credibility has collapsed and acceptance of commercial recommendations unchallenged.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the principal forgoes independent verification of an agent's representations in a domain where the agent's interests may diverge.

**Exemplar clusters:** c025, c075, c185, c199
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p25 — Payment Control and Segregation Failures

Failures in financial controls operating at transaction level: missing cross-checks against prior payments, segregation-of-duties absence, mis-set delegation limits, lapsed delegations at the moment of action, and contingency redirected without traceability.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is a defect in transaction-level financial control — cross-checks, segregation, delegation enforcement, or traceability of expenditure to authorisation.

**Exemplar clusters:** c003, c054, c074, c106, c154
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p26 — Cost Estimation and Budget Tracking Defects

Failures in which programme costs are not accurately scoped at outset or tracked during delivery: overhead-blind costing systems, missing comparative cost data, plan values consistently above benchmarks, and budget overruns discovered only retrospectively.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is a defect in how costs are estimated at design or monitored during delivery, producing systematic gaps between expected and actual spend.

**Exemplar clusters:** c030, c033, c064, c097
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p27 — Model and Reference Inputs Out of Date

Failures in which decision-supporting models, cost parameters, or published reference materials are not updated when underlying conditions change, so decisions and external users rely on inputs that no longer represent reality.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that an input artefact (model, parameter, guidance, reference document) becomes stale because no refresh process maintains alignment with current conditions.

**Exemplar clusters:** c040, c145
**Estimated population:** 1%

### p28 — Guidance and Procedure Inadequacy

Failures in which staff or stakeholders cannot act consistently because standard operating procedures are absent, draft, or overdue; published guidance is ambiguous; forms are confusingly designed; or terminology is loosely applied.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that authoritative guidance, procedure, or instructional artefacts are missing, ambiguous, or misapplied, leaving users without actionable direction.

**Exemplar clusters:** c008, c052, c093, c104
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p29 — Workforce Capacity and Coverage Shortfalls

Failures arising from staffing levels that are structurally inadequate for the work: critical-trade shortfalls, insufficient coverage relative to caseload or geography, overloaded individuals managing concurrent programmes, and bulk demand spikes overwhelming detection.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the quantum or distribution of staff capacity is structurally insufficient for the volume, geography, or simultaneity of required work.

**Exemplar clusters:** c058, c069, c153, c195
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p30 — Knowledge, Training, and Capability Erosion

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the knowledge or skill required to perform a function is not maintained — through turnover, training shortfall, recruitment policy, or loss of tool literacy.

**Exemplar clusters:** c024, c050, c070, c162
**Estimated population:** 1-3%

### p31 — Workforce Retention and Engagement Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that staff retention, engagement, or pipeline composition is degraded by systemic conditions of employment or recruitment design.

**Exemplar clusters:** c087, c113, c148, c160, c172
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p32 — Organisational Restructuring and Transition Disruption

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that organisational change disrupts ongoing delivery because tracking, funding, authority, or accountability arrangements were not preserved across the transition.

**Exemplar clusters:** c032, c039, c178, c183
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p33 — Inter-Agency Coordination Without Formal Mechanism

**Exemplar clusters:** c023, c035, c071, c108, c143
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p34 — Stakeholder Engagement and Outreach Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that engagement with beneficiaries or delivery partners is structurally insufficient or misdirected to support uptake, awareness, or implementation.

**Exemplar clusters:** c055, c080, c095, c164, c203
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p35 — Complaints and Feedback Channel Defects

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that complaints or feedback are not routed, aggregated, or resolved in a way that surfaces systemic problems to decision-makers.

**Exemplar clusters:** c041, c084
**Estimated population:** <1%

### p36 — Continuous Monitoring and Follow-Through Gaps

### p37 — Compliance Activity Misallocation

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that compliance resources are deployed in a manner not aligned with risk, producing systematic under-coverage of higher-priority targets.

**Exemplar clusters:** c026, c056, c083, c102
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p38 — Regulatory Lever and Sanction Inadequacy

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that an available regulatory or sanction lever is structurally incapable of, or institutionally not used for, driving compliance behaviour.

**Exemplar clusters:** c012, c038, c100, c117, c132, c137
**Estimated population:** 3-4%

### p39 — Regulatory Process Timeliness Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the regulatory process itself is too slow, or its detection design too easily evaded, to deliver intended deterrent effect within required timeframes.

**Exemplar clusters:** c013, c109, c116
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p40 — Funding Authority Misalignment with Mandate

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the legal, statutory, or funding authority needed for an action is absent, expired, or misaligned with the mandate the entity is trying to deliver.

**Exemplar clusters:** c006, c066, c125, c135, c149
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p41 — Procurement Timing and Sequencing Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that procurement activities are sequenced or timed inappropriately relative to the programme's launch or technical maturation requirements.

**Exemplar clusters:** c014, c073, c091, c136
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p42 — Acquisition Strategy and Technology Risk Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the strategy chosen for acquiring or operating capability does not match the inherent technical risk or operating-cost profile of what is being acquired.

**Exemplar clusters:** c120, c142, c158, c190
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p43 — Asset and Maintenance Lifecycle Neglect

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that decisions about physical asset upkeep, condition assessment, or lifecycle management are systematically deferred or under-managed.

**Exemplar clusters:** c060, c065, c147, c191
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p44 — Operational Readiness Masking Failures

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that headline capability indicators conceal structural unavailability of operational capacity to be deployed.

**Exemplar clusters:** c063, c094, c129
**Estimated population:** 1%

**Exemplar clusters:** c119, c163, c201, c202, c205, c207
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p46 — Policy Decision Instability Under Uncertainty

Failures in which policy positions are repeatedly revised, deferred, or held in suspense pending external events, or in which negotiated conditions are dropped before execution — producing operational gaps and lost enforceability.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is instability or incompleteness in upstream policy or agreement-formation that propagates downstream as operational gaps.

**Exemplar clusters:** c103, c175, c182, c188
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p47 — Centralisation/Decentralisation Structural Mismatch

Failures in which the chosen organisational structure (centralised or decentralised) is mismatched to the function: decentralised processing causing inconsistency, centralised recruiting losing localised follow-through, decentralised bargaining disadvantaging cohorts, or higher staffing ratios than peers driving cost.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the centralisation level of a function is misaligned with what the function requires for consistency, scale, or local responsiveness.

**Exemplar clusters:** c057, c148, c193, c196
**Estimated population:** 1-2%

### p48 — Equity and Distributional Failure of Programme Reach

Failures in which programme benefits or outcomes are distributed inequitably because of eligibility exclusions, structural EEO weaknesses, persistent representation gaps, or supply-side market failures preventing entitlement uptake.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that programme reach systematically excludes or under-serves cohorts that the programme intends or is required to serve equitably.

**Exemplar clusters:** c123, c159, c173, c194, c197
**Estimated population:** 2-3%

### p49 — Market Mechanism Assumption Failures

Failures in which a regulatory or programme design assumes a market behaviour that does not occur in practice — price caps becoming uniform prices, concentrated late lodgement, supply convergence, or reactive rather than proactive control establishment.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that the market or behavioural response assumed by the policy design does not materialise as expected, undermining the policy's intended effect.

**Exemplar clusters:** c122, c174, c200
**Estimated population:** 1%

### p50 — Successive Deferral Normalising Non-Delivery

Failures in which delivery shortfalls are obscured through rolling target deferrals, repeated baseline resets, or normalisation of unmet commitments across reporting cycles.

**Mechanism criterion:** The mechanism is that unmet delivery is administratively normalised by replacing missed targets with new future targets rather than acknowledging failure.

**Exemplar clusters:** c169, c199
**Estimated population:** <1%

## Notes

The 207 clusters cover an unusually broad mechanism space, requiring 50 parents to honour the tightness-over-breadth constraint. Several clusters could plausibly belong in two parents (e.g. c068 fits both p09 disclosure and p19 verification; c102 fits both p03 survey-bias and p37 compliance misallocation; c199 sits between p24 agent-dependence and p50 deferral). Where overlap exists, primary placement reflects the dominant mechanism. Parents are ordered into thematic blocks: measurement and information (p01-p09), governance and risk (p10-p15), design and timing (p16-p18), decision verification and consistency (p19-p20), procurement and contract (p21-p25), financial control and estimation (p25-p27), guidance and workforce (p28-p32), coordination and stakeholders (p33-p35), monitoring and regulation (p36-p39), authority and timing (p40-p44), financial drift and policy stability (p45-p46), and structural-distributional issues (p47-p50). Several very small clusters (n=3-5) anchor genuinely distinct mechanisms (e.g. c198 milestone-payment-certification conflict, c200 SG charge mechanics) and were preserved rather than absorbed into broader parents.
