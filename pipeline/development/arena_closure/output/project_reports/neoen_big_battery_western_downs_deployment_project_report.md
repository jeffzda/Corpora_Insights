# Neoen Big Battery (Western Downs) — Portfolio-Review Synthesis

## 1. Framing

The Western Downs Battery Energy Storage System (WDBESS) is a 200 MW / 400 MWh grid-scale battery in southern Queensland, supported by $21m of ARENA funding within a $243.88m total project value under the Advancing Renewables Program. It sits in ARENA's Large-Scale Battery Storage (LSBS) cohort — the funding round explicitly targeting the deployment of advanced (grid-forming, GFM) inverter capability at scale on the NEM. WDBESS's role in the portfolio is therefore not "another battery"; it is one of a handful of first-of-kind GFM deployments whose lessons are being mined to retire industry risk for the much larger pipeline behind it.

The evidence base spans 136 project records across 31 mechanism clusters and 20 parent archetypes, drawn from three publicly released documents: a Development Report (Milestone 2) published December 2024, Lessons Learnt Report 2 (February 2025), and Lessons Learnt Report 3 (January 2026). All records nominally carry project year 2023, but they describe a window stretching from the 2023 funding-and-procurement decisions through the 5.3.9 grid connection negotiation and Hold Point 1 testing in 2024–2025. The records cover the development, connection-application and early-commissioning stages — they do not yet cover steady-state operations.

## 2. The mechanism-family map

The footprint is heavily concentrated in regulatory, market-design and inverter-grid-interaction families. Five parent archetypes account for the bulk of what bit this project.

**Regulatory novelty and framework misfit (p23, p25, p26, p41).** This is the dominant story. The NER, the connection process, and the FCAS market specifications were all designed around grid-following (GFL) inverter behaviour, and WDBESS hit the seams when those instruments had to accommodate GFM. In [c903] the project absorbed a direct revenue hit when NER v218 (December 2024) broadened the definition of inertia to include synthetic contributions, which AEMO then used to reduce the Very Fast FCAS enablement registered for WDBESS during the 5.3.9 transition<sup>1,2,3,4</sup>. The change landed *after* the project had committed and was already underway, so the FCAS revenue forecast at financial close no longer holds<sup>2</sup>. In [c682] the project flags that the magnitude of the hit is still unknown, because AEMO has not finalised its synthetic-inertia treatment<sup>5</sup>. In [c874] the same regulatory immaturity manifested as a post-registration s5.3.9 process: AEMO and Powerlink could not assess GFM 1-second FCAS contributions cleanly and reached an interim arrangement to register 5 MW below the calculated value, with the door left open to revisit<sup>6,7</sup>. In [c679] the s5.3.4A(b)(1A) "no less onerous" rule turned into a backwards-looking constraint, because GFM reactive settling times are longer than the GFL baseline being used as the floor<sup>8</sup>. And in [c007] the standard architecture pushes frequency control through the Power Plant Controller rather than natively at the inverter terminals, imposing a communications delay that capable hardware cannot otherwise overcome<sup>9</sup>.

**Value capture blocked by market structure (p22).** Three records in [c739] make the point that there is no NEM market that prices GFM services or synthetic inertia, so grant funding (rather than revenue) had to do the heavy lifting at FID<sup>10,11,12</sup>. [c942] adds the corollary: the System Strength Charge avoidance was only realised because the SSC rule change post-dated WDBESS's commitment, so the GFM premium is hard to justify on its own merits going forward<sup>13</sup>.

**Inverter-based-resource grid-interaction failure (p15).** Five clusters here, four of them harmonic-flavoured. The dominant narrative is that switching from GFL to GFM altered the WDBESS harmonic emission profile [c1019], and this collided with a concurrent change in Powerlink's harmonic assessment methodology and updated allocations and polygon data, producing a potential exceedance at the 14th harmonic<sup>14,15,16</sup>. The downstream consequences cascade: [c1053] flags that future projects should design in harmonic filter contingencies, because the GFL→GFM transition can trigger a need for filtering<sup>17,18</sup>; [c559] notes the exceedance risk is not static — neighbouring connections can shift the resonance picture<sup>19</sup>; [c612] surfaces that the faster GFM control loop changes fault-current behaviour, requiring redesign of protection and arc-flash settings within the BoP<sup>20</sup>; and [c740] frames protection redesign as one of the headline time/effort sinks for the project<sup>21</sup>. [c788] adds the upstream geographic story: the Available Fault Level at the Western Downs node is forecast to fall to zero in the short term as planned VRE crowds in, even though no formal system-strength shortfall has been declared<sup>22</sup>.

**Process and planning friction (p24, p27, p36, p38).** The connection process generated iterative rework [c681]: Tesla updated GFM harmonic data in late 2023; Powerlink released a new IBR power-quality methodology in January 2024; new harmonic allocations and polygon data arrived in August 2024 — three months *into* the 5.3.9 studies — forcing rebase<sup>23,24</sup>. The opacity of the NSP allocation process makes early defensive scoping impossible<sup>24</sup>. [c500] and [c1053] therefore land on the same recommendation: reserve site footprint, electrical design margin and budget for a harmonic filter even if not initially required<sup>25,18</sup>. The asymmetry between the GFL stage 1 (no filter needed) and the GFM transition (filter possible) is a textbook [c645] sequential revelation: resolving the primary GFL design hid the GFM-specific failure mode<sup>26,27</sup>. [c779] and [c1081] capture the procurement coupling: in the Australian BESS market EPC contractors typically bring their own PPC/BMS, so the connection package cannot be finalised until the EPC is selected, which serialises rather than parallelises the critical path<sup>28,29</sup>.

**Knowledge gaps and OEM opacity (p05).** [c536] reads as the optimistic side: AEMO/NSP familiarity with GFM models is expected to improve as more LSBS projects come through, easing future timelines<sup>30</sup>. [c022] is the pessimistic side: PSS/E and PSCAD model capability and compliance with the Power System Model Guidelines remain weak points whenever a new GFM model first hits 5.3.4/5.3.9<sup>31</sup>.

Other parents (p06 cycling cost of synthetic inertia<sup>32</sup>; p07 retrofit space exhaustion<sup>33,34</sup>; p11 GFM tuning complexity<sup>35</sup>; p17 OEM-by-OEM heterogeneity in synthetic inertia<sup>36</sup>; p20 GFM capex premium<sup>37</sup>; p39 long-lead harmonic filter<sup>38</sup>; p48 QLD inertia deficit<sup>39</sup>; p49 neighbour-driven study refresh<sup>40</sup>; p01 NSP data inaccessibility<sup>41</sup>) each show up once but support the wider narrative.

## 3. Within-tech reference class

**[c1019] Harmonic profile changes with operational mode.** WDBESS is the canonical GFL→GFM-transition case in this cluster. The Origin Mortlake peer (Battery storage) makes the same systemic argument that filter-driven cost and schedule could decelerate the transition<sup>42</sup>; the University of Wollongong harmonic study confirms the methodological problem (allocation conservatism, summation-law misfit) at theoretical level. The Ekistica grid-forming portfolio synthesis explicitly cites WDBESS as the worked example of the GFL-to-GFM filter problem. Manifestation here is *typical for first-of-kind GFM batteries* but more acute than for GFL-only peers, which were not exposed to the operational-mode-shift dimension.

**[c874] Post-registration s5.3.9 for GFM ambiguity.** The closest peer is AGL Broken Hill — five records describe an essentially identical pattern: voluntary GFM specifications imposed at registration, post-registration s5.3.9 negotiation about voltage-source bandwidth and angle realignment timeframes. Darlington Point shows the same dynamic in NSW. WDBESS's experience is therefore *the modal LSBS-cohort experience*, not an outlier — the cohort is collectively absorbing the cost of NER incompleteness for GFM.

**[c903] Rule change reducing existing project value.** This is a recurring NEM pattern: Lake Bonney was hit by the PFR mandate and PFR Incentive Arrangements transition; Gannawarra was hit by ESS scheduling classification; ESCRI by 5MS; Hornsdale by FCAS constraint relaxation post-system-separation. WDBESS's instance — synthetic-inertia counted against FCAS enablement — is novel in form but *typical in pattern*: the regulatory floor moved during the project's commitment window.

**[c739] Absent market for advanced services.** Highly typical for the LSBS cohort. ESCRI, Hornsdale Power Reserve Upgrade, Wallgrove and Mortlake all flag the absence of paid markets for inertia, system strength or advanced inverter services. WDBESS is consistent with the peer pattern; the persistent gap is the cohort-level signal, not project-specific.

**[c681] Connection requirement uncertainty.** Lake Bonney, Gannawarra and ESCRI all logged this same friction during their connection processes (FSSIA introduction, system-strength assessment changes, NSP iterations). The WDBESS variant is distinctive in that the iteration came from harmonic methodology and allocation updates *during* studies — a tighter, more procedural variant than the more architectural reworks at earlier batteries.

**[c612] GFM fault contribution and arc flash.** Direct alignment with Neoen Victorian Big Battery (Moorabool) and Neoen Blyth — both Neoen sister projects flag the same GFM fault-contribution effect, and both initially scoped on the assumption that no hardware change would be needed. WDBESS is a third instance of the same Neoen-portfolio learning. Cluster signal is now strong enough that it should be a default scoping assumption for any GFM (retrofit or greenfield) project.

**[c1001] GFM tuning complexity.** Strong peer alignment with AGL Broken Hill, Carwarp, Wallgrove and Darlington Point; this cluster reads as the empirical confirmation that GFM tuning is materially more demanding than GFL.

**[c779]/[c1081] EPC/PPC coupling.** The peer is Neoen Blyth, which flags an identical pattern. This is plausibly a feature of the *current* Australian BESS supply chain rather than a permanent constraint.

**[c554] Retrofit space exhaustion.** The WDBESS variant — no space pre-reserved for a harmonic filter — is structurally similar to the SETuP/Daly River BESS retrofit constraints, but at utility scale. The peer-tech precedent is thin for utility batteries specifically; WDBESS is therefore an early data point for the cohort.

## 4. Cross-cutting observations

The most consequential intersection is **regulatory framework gap × IBR grid-interaction × procurement timing**. The harmonic-filter risk did not arise from a single failure mode: it arose because (i) GFM changes the inverter's harmonic profile, (ii) Powerlink simultaneously revised its assessment methodology and allocations, (iii) the revisions arrived three months into the 5.3.9 studies, (iv) the original site was designed without filter footprint, and (v) the EPC coupling means filter procurement could not be parallelised. None of these alone would have been disabling; their compound is what creates the schedule and capex exposure flagged in [c554], [c1053], [c500] and [c004]<sup>33,17,25,38</sup>.

A second compound is **rule change × revenue forecasting**. The NER v218 inertia redefinition, the still-unresolved AEMO synthetic-inertia treatment, and the absence of a paid inertia market combine to make the GFM revenue side genuinely speculative<sup>1,5,11</sup>. ARENA grant funding is doing the work that the market does not yet do<sup>10,12,13</sup>; if the regulatory interpretation tightens further, the cohort-level case for grant-funded GFM premium has to be re-argued from system-benefit grounds, not from project IRR.

A third is **GFM control behaviour × protection design × arc-flash safety**. The faster GFM control loop changes fault current characteristics, which propagates into protection setting redesign, which propagates into arc-flash incident energy and potentially new hardware (inter-trip schemes, relay reviews), which collides with the assumption — made at WDBESS, VBB and Blyth alike — that GFM is "firmware only"<sup>20,21</sup>. This intersection is now well-evidenced enough across the Neoen portfolio to be a default planning assumption.

## 5. Time trajectory

The records line up cleanly across roughly 18 months of project history. The Development Report (Milestone 2, December 2024) lays out the GFM-deployment risk taxonomy as anticipated risks at the connection-application stage — protection redesign, GFM tuning, market absence, OEM heterogeneity, capex premium<sup>11,12,20,21,31,35,36,37</sup>. Lessons Learnt Report 2 (February 2025) is the operationalised version: the harmonic-filter risk has crystallised as a real exceedance at the 14th harmonic, the NSP data opacity is now framed as a structural problem, and retrofit space is identified as a hard constraint<sup>14,15,16,23,24,33</sup>. Lessons Learnt Report 3 (January 2026) closes the loop on the FCAS revenue side: NER v218, AEMO's synthetic-inertia interpretation, and the post-HP1 registration outcome materialise the revenue downside that earlier reports had flagged as a risk<sup>1,2,3,4,6,32</sup>.

Persistence vs resolution: the **harmonic-filter problem persists** in latent form — design contingencies are the recommended mitigation but the underlying NSP-process opacity is unresolved<sup>24,25</sup>. The **FCAS-revenue impact persists** and deepens — the magnitude is not bounded because AEMO has not landed a final position<sup>5</sup>. The **GFM registration ambiguity persists** but is at least operationally containable through interim arrangements<sup>6</sup>. The **protection/arc-flash issue resolves** via redesign within the BoP — pattern is now well-understood, even if it adds work<sup>20,21</sup>.

## 6. What this implies for analogous future projects

For any portfolio manager assessing the next GFM battery proposal under broadly similar 2024–2026 NEM conditions:

- **Treat GFM as a hardware-and-firmware change, not firmware-only.** The protection-redesign and arc-flash hardware footprint shows up at WDBESS, VBB and Blyth — three Neoen projects, same pattern<sup>20,21</sup>. Greenfield designs should reserve site space and cable-route provision for a harmonic filter even if first-stage studies do not require one<sup>25,33</sup>.
- **Price in registration uncertainty on the FCAS side.** WDBESS's experience with synthetic inertia being counted against Very Fast FCAS enablement<sup>1,3,4,6</sup> is likely to recur until AEMO finalises its synthetic-inertia treatment. Revenue cases that depend materially on Very Fast FCAS should stress-test against AEMO's evolving interpretation rather than a static MASS reading.
- **Sequence the 5.3.9 process around NSP data drops, not around internal milestones.** WDBESS lost three months of study work to a Powerlink allocation/methodology update mid-process<sup>23</sup>; the structural fix (NSP transparency) is outside the proponent's control, but defensive scheduling is not<sup>24</sup>.
- **Be honest about GFM revenue.** The grant component is doing real work that the market does not<sup>10,11,12</sup>. The disappearance of SSC at WDBESS was a timing accident<sup>13</sup>; future projects should not bank on that.
- **EPC/PPC procurement coupling is a critical-path item.** Connection package finalisation and EPC selection are serialised in the current Australian market<sup>28,29</sup>; either accept the schedule consequence or do the work upfront to make inverter-OEM-native PPCs acceptable to EPC contractors.

Time-bound dependencies: every one of these recommendations is conditioned on the NER state circa 2024–2026, the Powerlink IBR methodology of January 2024, AEMO's still-evolving synthetic-inertia interpretation, and the present concentration of EPC supply. As more LSBS projects clear the connection process, [c536]'s familiarity dividend should compress some of these timelines<sup>30</sup>.

## 7. Open questions and evidence gaps

The corpus is rich on connection and pre-commissioning friction but has nothing yet on operational performance. Specific gaps:

- No throughput/efficiency measurement for the synthetic-inertia provision flagged in [c622]<sup>32</sup> — the cycling cost of GFM is asserted, not yet quantified for this asset.
- No quantified FCAS revenue outcome — Lessons Learnt 3 indicates the impact is unknown<sup>5</sup>; an operational report would close this.
- No resolution on whether a harmonic filter actually gets installed — the corpus stops at "potentially required, hard to retrofit"<sup>14,15,33</sup>.
- No data on the Available Fault Level trajectory at the Western Downs node<sup>22</sup> as further VRE commits — relevant for the next QLD GFM connection in this region.
- The peer-tech reference class for utility-scale BESS retrofit space constraints [c554] is thin; whether WDBESS is exceptional or representative is not yet inferable.
- The 93 unclustered residuals (more than two-thirds of the project's records) likely contain operational, financial and stakeholder material that is not surfaced here. A targeted re-extraction once commissioning records are published would materially sharpen the picture.
