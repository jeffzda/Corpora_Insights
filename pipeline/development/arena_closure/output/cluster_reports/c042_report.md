# Electrode Material Degradation From Chemical Incompatibility: A Cross-Portfolio Synthesis

## 1. Opening framing

At its core, this archetype is a story about materials being asked to do a job in a chemical environment they cannot survive. An electrode (or, more broadly, a wetted surface that carries current or contains a reactive fluid) is selected for its electrochemical function — conductivity, catalytic activity, work function, optical properties — but the species it must contact in service attack it: corroding it, dissolving it, embrittling it, poisoning its catalytic sites, or forming parasitic phases that destroy the structure it depends on. The device works initially; then it degrades on a timescale shorter than the asset is meant to last, and often shorter than the project's own demonstration window.

This matters disproportionately for an ARENA-style portfolio because almost every decarbonisation pathway ARENA funds — green hydrogen, direct electrochemical reduction of iron, perovskite and tandem PV, concentrated solar thermal, advanced batteries, biofuels, hydrogen blending in gas networks — depends on putting novel materials into contact with aggressive species (concentrated caustic, halides, hydrogen, supercritical CO₂, molten salts, biogas contaminants, mobile organic ions). The cluster contains 150 records across 56 projects and 8 ARENA categories, with project years spanning 2010–2024 and publish dates running through to early 2026. That breadth tells us this is not a quirk of one technology generation: it is a structural feature of the technologies ARENA exists to de-risk.

## 2. The mechanism

The causal pathway is consistent across very different technologies. A novel device requires a material with a specific electrochemical or photoelectronic property. The candidates with the right property are a small set; the ones with the right *property and* full chemical robustness in the operating environment are a smaller set still, often empty. Designers proceed with the best property-side candidate and accept a chemical-stability gap, sometimes explicitly, sometimes by inheritance from lab-scale work where exposure durations were short.

In service, one or more degradation modes then activate: (i) bulk corrosion or dissolution of the electrode<sup>1,2,3</sup>; (ii) interfacial reactions producing passivating or insulating layers that suppress the intended electrochemistry<sup>4,5</sup>; (iii) diffusion of electrode atoms into adjacent functional layers, where they act as recombination centres or dopants<sup>6,7,8</sup>; (iv) attack on the binder, encapsulant, seal, or substrate that holds the electrode in place, causing delamination<sup>9,10,11</sup>; or (v) catalytic poisoning by trace contaminants from upstream processes<sup>12,13</sup>. Each pathway shortens device life or compresses the operating window below what the techno-economics assumed.

What distinguishes this archetype from adjacent ones (mechanical fatigue, thermal cycling, manufacturing defects) is that the failure is *intrinsic to the chemistry of operation* rather than to a fault, transient, or environmental excursion. It would occur in a perfectly built, perfectly operated device. That is why it is so corrosive, in the portfolio sense: it cannot be engineered away by quality control or careful operation alone — it requires either a different material, a different chemistry, or a barrier strategy.

The mechanism is largely *time-invariant* in its physics. What is time-variant is the set of materials available, the maturity of barrier and coating technologies, and the cost of the workarounds. A 2010 caustic-corrosion problem and a 2024 caustic-corrosion problem share a mechanism but sit in different solution landscapes.

## 3. How it manifests across the portfolio

The richest single concentration of evidence is the [Fortescue – low temperature direct electrochemical reduction] project, whose 2024–2026 reports systematically exhausted the candidate space for cathode materials, structural alloys, polymer binders and membranes in 50 wt% NaOH at ~100 °C. Iron and copper cathodes corroded outright<sup>1</sup>; titanium passivated and ceased to reduce iron<sup>4,14</sup>; nickel suppressed HER but not in a useful direction<sup>15</sup>; 316 stainless steel corroded at 0.27–0.5 mm/year and contaminated the iron product with Ni and Cr<sup>2,16</sup>; mixed NaOH/KOH electrolytes destabilised performance and corroded Ti at high KOH concentration<sup>17,18</sup>. In parallel, the polymer-matrix electrodes failed by two coupled mechanisms — chemical attack of the binder by the electrolyte, and exceedance of the binder's glass-transition temperature — producing catastrophic debonding within hours<sup>9,19,20</sup>. Membranes shed zirconia fibres and salts into the cell<sup>21</sup>. Even the broader plant context surfaced caustic stress-corrosion cracking risk for steels<sup>22</sup>, refractory damage from sodium ingress into downstream EAF/BOF furnaces<sup>23</sup>, and dissolution of gangue minerals (silica, alumina) into the electrolyte where they then inhibit the reduction pathway<sup>24,25</sup>. This is essentially a complete catalogue of the archetype in a single project, and a useful illustration that for any given chemistry there is rarely a single failure mode but a *web* of incompatibilities to be mapped.

The perovskite PV strand in [ACAP], [Development of Stable Electrodes for Perovskite Solar Cells], [Monolithic Perovskite-Silicon Tandem Cells] and [Manufacturing of Printed Perovskite PV Modules] shows the same archetype on much smaller length scales but with comparable severity. Gold rear electrodes diffuse into the perovskite layer<sup>26,27</sup>; silver rear electrodes are attacked by mobile iodide<sup>28,29</sup>; copper reacts with the perovskite at grain boundaries and shrinks the lattice<sup>30</sup>; lithium dopants in the Spiro-OMeTAD HTL are themselves mobile and destabilise the cell<sup>31</sup>; the very encapsulants the cells need (EVA in particular) react with the perovskite<sup>32</sup>; and direct printing of carbon-based electrodes degrades the absorber via ink solvents<sup>33</sup>. The Spiro-OMeTAD/iodide oxidation pathway has been pinned, in 2021 vintage records, as the dominant degradation route in semitransparent and tandem cells via series-resistance increase<sup>34</sup>. More recent (2024–2025) records on TOPCon and HJT cells show the same archetype expressed through soldering-flux residues, NaCl, and moisture-driven electrochemistry corroding metallisation under damp-heat<sup>35,36,37</sup> — a manufacturing-era manifestation distinct from the older "intrinsic perovskite" version, and one that has implications for module bankability in coastal Australian conditions.

A third strand is high-temperature heat-transfer and power-conversion fluids. The [Solar-Driven Supercritical CO₂ Brayton Cycle] project (project year 2011, published 2017) found that supercritical CO₂ acts as a solvent on PTFE-based seal materials and elastomers, dissolving binders and swelling polymers so that seals do not re-seat after a single depressurisation cycle<sup>38,39,40</sup>. [ASTRI] documented sodium-side weld degradation in 316L/Inconel 625 at 750 °C<sup>41</sup>, the corrosivity of chloride-salt heat exchangers<sup>42</sup>, and PCM leaching out of geopolymer encapsulation shells<sup>43</sup>. [Advanced Solar Thermal Energy Storage] (project year 2010) had already found that nitrate, tertiary, and quaternary salt systems could not survive above 600 °C and that carbonate alternatives, even if thermally stable, would aggressively attack containment alloys<sup>44,45</sup>.

The hydrogen strand exhibits the archetype across the value chain. III-V photoelectrochemical absorbers photo-corrode within hours in electrolyte<sup>46</sup>; alkaline electrolyser cathodes designed for caustic stability are destroyed by acidic biomass-processing reactor effluent at the [Waste Biomass to Renewable Hydrogen] interface<sup>13,47</sup>; nickel SOFC anodes form nickel nitrides on exposure to ammonia at intermediate temperatures during start-up/shutdown in [Cavendish]<sup>48,49</sup>; pipeline steels are subject to hydrogen embrittlement at concentrations above ~20%<sup>50,51,52</sup>; deteriorated wellbore cement is more permeable in hydrogen service<sup>53</sup>; and Hazer's catalytic biogas reformer is poisoned by H₂S<sup>54</sup>. The [Cooper Basin Enhanced Geothermal Systems] casing failure in 2009 — caustic stress-corrosion cracking of 150-ksi V150 production casing due to unset cement creating a high-pH annulus — is the archetype's most consequential single event in the corpus, sitting at the intersection of material selection (high-strength but caustic-susceptible alloy), unintended chemistry (alkaline cement annulus), and mechanical loading (cyclic thermal stress)<sup>55,56</sup>.

A fourth strand involves ancillary materials — seals, masking tapes, lubricants, glassware, encapsulants, masking films — whose incompatibility with process chemistry surfaces only at scale or only after long exposure<sup>57,58,59,60,61</sup>. These are nearly invisible in feasibility studies but consume real time on pilot lines, as the [SunDrive] commercial-scale deployment demonstrated when systemic corrosion of copper plant wiring became a yield-limiting issue<sup>62,63</sup>.

A clear temporal sub-pattern: older records (2013–2018) tend to identify the archetype as a single material-pair problem ("gold diffuses", "PTFE swells in CO₂", "nitrate salts decompose"). Newer records (2022–2026) are markedly more systemic — they describe interaction matrices, multiple coupled failure modes, and the emergence of incompatibility at scale-up rather than at lab scale (e.g., [Fortescue], [SunDrive Commercialisation], [Cavendish], [Multiphase electrolysers]<sup>64</sup>). This evolution is at least partly an artefact of better instrumentation and post-mortem methodology, but it is also a consequence of more projects reaching the pilot/commercial scale where the full chemistry of a real plant — including impurities from upstream, transient operating states, and recycle accumulation — is finally exposed.

## 4. Project-level context

Project economics shape which materials get chosen, and therefore which version of the archetype shows up. [SunDrive] exists *because* gold and silver are unaffordable at TW scale; copper is the only economical metallisation, but copper introduces the diffusion-and-oxidation suite of failures that the project then has to engineer around<sup>65,66,67</sup>. [Fortescue's] DER process specifically requires 50 wt% NaOH at 100 °C to access the desired electrochemistry; the chemistry is non-negotiable, so the entire materials program is downstream of that constraint. [Cooper Basin] used 150-ksi casing because of well depth and pressure requirements; that material choice was load-driven, and the caustic environment was an unintended and unrecognised side-effect of cementing practice<sup>56</sup>. In each case the archetype is not a contingent error but a structural consequence of the project's value proposition. This is important for portfolio-level reasoning: where the value proposition forces a chemistry, the materials problem is not optional and must be funded explicitly.

## 5. Event-level context

Event siblings reinforce two points. First, materials-degradation events almost never appear alone — they cluster with capital-cost events<sup>68</sup>, with scale-up-translation events, with encapsulation-failure events<sup>69</sup>, and with sequential-problem-revelation events where solving one constraint exposes the next<sup>70,71</sup>. The [SunDrive Commercialisation] event neatly illustrates this: the corrosion finding sits beside an "operating conditions accelerate degradation beyond manufacturer specifications" sibling and a "passive asset management causes unpredicted failures" sibling<sup>72,73</sup>. Together they tell a coherent story about a pilot line where chemical incompatibility, inadequate inspection, and over-trust in vendor specs co-produced yield instability. Second, event siblings show that the archetype is rarely diagnosed at first encounter — the [Fortescue] and [Next-Gen Selective Emitters] events both required iterative investigation before the chemical root cause was isolated, with the team rejecting hypotheses about surface treatment or paste behaviour before settling on the underlying chemistry<sup>74</sup>.

## 6. Temporal trajectory

The mechanism's *physics* has not changed across the 2010–2024 project-year range. What has changed is (i) the diagnostic resolution available — operando Raman, KPFM, TIMA, EIS-based degradation deconvolution all appear in post-2020 records and were largely unavailable in 2013-vintage work; (ii) the population of candidate materials, particularly for HTLs in perovskites, polysilicon-passivating contacts, and Cd-free buffer layers in CIGS, all of which now provide alternatives that were not yet credible a decade ago; and (iii) the deployment scale, which has surfaced incompatibilities (recycle accumulation, soldering-flux residues, manufacturing-line corrosion) that lab-scale work suppressed.

For older projects (CSIRO sCO₂ 2011, Advanced Solar Thermal 2010, Cooper Basin 2010, Collinsville 2013), the conclusions about what *cannot* be done with available materials probably still hold; what may have changed is the cost-effectiveness of mitigations. For newer projects (Fortescue 2024, Cavendish 2024, Multiphase electrolysers 2024, Hydrogen powder export 2024), we are seeing the archetype in real time and should expect the materials-selection narratives to continue evolving in subsequent reports.

## 7. Mitigation patterns

Five families of mitigation recur. First, *substitution* — replacing the offending material with one of comparable function and superior chemical robustness (Cd-free buffer layers, NiOx replacing Spiro-OMeTAD, Mg(OH)₂ replacing lime, nickel-bonded WC replacing cobalt-bonded WC<sup>75</sup>, IZO/MoOx stacks replacing gold<sup>76</sup>). Second, *barrier and coating* strategies — overcoating Ag plasmonic particles with MgF₂<sup>77,78</sup>, capping Cu with Sn/Ag for solderability<sup>79</sup>, AZO/TiO₂ barrier layers between metals and silicon. Third, *upstream conditioning* — pH adjustment between BPR and FEC<sup>13</sup>, water purification before electrolysers<sup>80</sup>, H₂S removal before Hazer reactors<sup>54</sup>, sodium washing of DER product before EAF<sup>23</sup>. Fourth, *operational envelope restriction* — avoiding intermediate temperatures where NH₃-Ni nitridation is favoured<sup>48</sup>, restricting anolyte/catholyte chemistries. Fifth, *over-specification* — duplex/super-duplex/Inconel for caustic plants<sup>22</sup>, more expensive PEEK fabrics in membranes.

The effectiveness of substitution mitigations is itself time-variant: alternatives that were "research curiosities" in 2015 are now industrially available, and the mitigation cost has fallen accordingly. Barrier-coating mitigations are robust over time but introduce series resistance and process-complexity penalties. Upstream conditioning is robust but adds capex and stream complexity. Over-specification is robust but breaks the economics of low-cost technology — which is often the original ARENA value proposition.

## 8. Implications for portfolio decisions

A portfolio manager should treat materials-compatibility risk as *always present* in any project that puts a novel functional material into a reactive operating environment, and should be especially alert when the value proposition forces a chemistry (high-concentration caustic, supercritical CO₂, mobile-ion perovskites, hydrogen at pressure, ammonia at intermediate temperature). Three concrete adjustments follow.

First, scope diligence to require an explicit *materials-compatibility map* — not just an electrode-active-material assessment but the full wetted-surface inventory including binders, seals, gaskets, membranes, encapsulants, and pipework — with named failure modes and time-to-failure estimates. The Fortescue final report is the model here: it is the kind of artefact that should be produced earlier in a project's life, not at the end.

Second, build into milestone gates an expectation of *long-duration exposure tests* on representative coupons, not just short-cycle electrochemical tests. Many of the failures in this corpus only appeared after 24–500 hours of exposure, well beyond the duration of typical performance demonstrations.

Third, recognise that mitigation costs will eat margin and should be costed in upfront. The "drop-in cheap material" theory of cost reduction (e.g. copper for silver, NaOH for KOH, PTFE for engineered elastomers) almost always understates lifetime cost. Where the mitigation is over-specification of containment alloys, the project is no longer a low-cost project.

Where the evidence base is older (sCO₂ 2011, Cooper Basin 2010, Advanced Solar Thermal 2010), current applicability should be tested by a small bench update before relying on those findings to inform new investments — barrier coatings and alloy availability have moved on.

## 9. Open questions and evidence gaps

The corpus is strong on *identification* of the mechanism and weaker on *quantitative degradation rates under representative duty cycles*. Few records report extrapolated lifetime in years for a specific materials choice, and fewer still tie that to economic lifetime assumptions in the project's business case. The archetype is also under-represented in operational monitoring data: most records are from R&D or pilot phases, and we have little evidence on how these failure modes propagate into commercial operations and warranty claims for assets ARENA has co-funded. There is a temporal gap on intermediate-scale demonstration outcomes — many projects either report lab findings (2013–2020 vintage) or full-pilot findings (2022–2026 vintage), with relatively little on the 100 kW–10 MW scale-up phase where these failure modes typically first bite. Finally, the corpus tells us almost nothing about *recyclability and end-of-life chemistry* of the very materials whose in-service incompatibility has been catalogued — a gap that becomes more salient as first-generation perovskite and copper-metallised modules approach decommissioning. Closing these gaps would meaningfully sharpen the picture for portfolio decisions over the next funding cycle.
