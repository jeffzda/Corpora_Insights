# Reference-class memo: hydrogen-from-biomass FOAK demonstration

**Hypothetical project.** A first-of-a-kind 5 MW hydrogen-from-biomass demonstration plant in regional Australia. Novel feedstock-to-syngas pathway. Single-site demo intended to inform commercial scale-up.

**Question.** What does the ARENA portfolio say about how projects like this systematically run into trouble?

**Method.** Map the project's risk surface onto the v2 parent-archetype layer (71 mechanism families, derived from 1,141 clusters / 90,192 records). For each parent that plausibly bites a project of this profile, pull cluster-level reference-class evidence and the most-on-domain illustrative record.

The 15 parents below cover **306 clusters / 5,808 records** of reference-class evidence. Every one of them has a hydrogen or bioenergy record in its top cluster — this corpus is dense in projects with at least one structural axis in common with this hypothetical.

## What the reference class predicts for this project

The corpus says — empirically, with population counts — that a hydrogen-from-biomass FOAK demo of this profile should expect to encounter:

1. **Feedstock-handling will surprise you.** Biomass batch variability is the single strongest signal in the reference class for feedstock-driven projects (p50 has *Biomass and Biological Feedstock Variability Limits Scale-Up* as cluster #2). Plan for feedstock characterisation budget upfront; expect bridging/flow blockages in handling equipment.

2. **The regulatory novelty cost is double.** Both p23 (regulatory framework gap) and p25 (policy uncertainty) are heavily populated *with hydrogen records specifically*. Denham is a worked example of "first application of its kind" approvals taking longer than anyone scoped. Plan a dedicated regulatory work-stream that runs ahead of the technical schedule, not behind it.

3. **Front-end scoping under novelty is the biggest single bucket.** p36 has 755 records and the top cluster (*Insufficient Technical Scoping…* c500, n=214) is the third-largest cluster in the corpus. Denham's "we didn't realise we needed a water cooling tower" is exactly this. Most pre-FID effort spent here pays back.

4. **Commissioning is where surprises actually surface.** p38 (270 records) is dominated by *commissioning exposing minor failures* and *sequential problem revelation*. Build commissioning float into the schedule; don't promise an aggressive PPA-start date you'll have to renegotiate.

5. **Bankability and offtake are not solvable inside the project.** p43, p22, p20 together carry 1,444 records of evidence that hydrogen projects fail to clear cost-benefit thresholds and can't monetise produced H₂ in nascent markets. ARENA grant funding closes part of this gap; the rest needs offtake commitments before FID, not after.

6. **The energy-balance penalty for H₂ storage/transport is real and quantified.** p64's *Hydrogen Storage and Transport Consumes Large Energy Fraction* — up to 50% in the cited record — should set the ceiling on what "useful net output" can plausibly mean. If your case relies on shipping H₂ off-site, model this loss explicitly.

7. **Workforce constraints are structural, not transitional.** p34's *Novel Technology Unfamiliarity Causes Local Maintenance Failure* explicitly says regional H₂ projects can't realistically train local maintenance — Denham's resolution was vendor O&M contracts. Plan and price this from the beginning, not as a fall-back.

8. **Lab-to-field at 5 MW will reveal latent variables.** p16's *Pilot Site Characteristics Limit Solution Transferability* has 74 records of "what worked at small scale didn't survive contact with the real site." Allow a budget line for redesign rounds during commissioning, not just for the build.

## How to use this memo

For each of the 15 parents, the v2 layer offers three drill-down paths:

- **Population sense-making**: how big is this risk in the portfolio? — answer is the row count.
- **Sub-mechanism enumeration**: *what specifically* goes wrong inside the family? — answer is the cluster names.
- **Evidence retrieval**: who has reported it? — answer is `tools/q cluster c###` returns 100+ records per cluster with project, document, narrative.

A sceptical reader can verify any claim above by drilling into the cluster and reading the records. The memo's synthesis is an interpretation; the substrate underneath is the record-level evidence.

## Caveats

- **The 15 parents are my interpretive scoping.** A different analyst would route differently. p17 (heterogeneity), p41 (standards/cert), p65 (complexity) are reasonable additions; some I included (p25 policy uncertainty) might be downgraded in a different framing.
- **Population counts mix tech.** The 595 records under p23 are not all hydrogen. The illustrative records show the corpus *has* hydrogen evidence under each parent, but the cluster's total record count includes other tech. This is a feature for reference-class reasoning (you want to learn from analogous projects across tech) but it means counts can't be naively read as "595 hydrogen-regulatory records."
- **Cluster-name quality is doing real work.** The reader is trusting that the cluster's name accurately summarises its records. Spot-checks on three random members give the right confidence level.
- **One memo isn't a formal probability assessment.** A real ARENA risk-rating would weight these 15 parents against the project's specific design decisions. This memo gives the *priors* the project should be evaluated against — not the *posterior*.

## What this demonstrates about the v2 substrate

The reference-class memo is exactly the use case that motivated building the parent layer — it answers a specific portfolio-decision question by routing it through pre-extracted, pre-categorised mechanism families. RAG can retrieve passages relevant to "hydrogen biomass demo failure"; it cannot retrieve a *categorised reference class* with population counts and pre-named sub-mechanisms. That distinction is the v2 contribution.
